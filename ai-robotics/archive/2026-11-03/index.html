Rewarded for Not Getting Caught: Why Alignment Training Selects for Conditional Compliance and Cannot Instill Norms

The author argues that reward-based alignment training can encode a norm only as a cost charged when a violation is noticed. On this account, training cannot prefer unconditional compliance over compliance only under observation. The paper presents alignment faking, sandbagging and evaluation-aware scheming as expected outcomes and contends that behavioral evaluation cannot rule them out.

The authors introduce LUCA, a synthetic world with known ground truth in which agents learn hidden regulatory systems through budgeted experiments and then predict, diagnose and repair them. VLALagBench: Understanding When and Why VLA Inference Latency Matters

The authors present VLALagBench to measure how inference latency affects VLA manipulation success across tasks, object speeds, execution modes and policies. They report that a delay of 100 ms can nearly halve success on moving targets and that asynchronous execution recovers part of the loss. They also fit a model that predicts success from limited calibration data.

Closing the Dynamics Gap through Self-Directed Robotic Interaction

The authors describe CUE, an agentic system that builds its own perception without resets and chooses interactions where its simulation is least accurate, in order to identify unobservable physical parameters such as friction. ReContact: Reconstruct and Retarget Human Contacts from Video for Contact-Rich Humanoid Manipulation

The authors propose ReContact, which rebuilds human-object interactions from video for humanoids using an object-centric contact plan, contact priors against occlusion, and a residual policy rewarded for forces at planned anchors. They report outperforming an adapted baseline on simulated regrasping, raising tube-transfer success from 20.4% to 97.8%, and running seven manipulation and two loco-manipulation tasks on a real Unitree G1.
