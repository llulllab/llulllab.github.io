arXiv · cs.RO16 November 2026

ForceHarness: Turning Force into Action and Physics for Frozen Frontier Models

The authors present ForceHarness, which lets API-only frozen multimodal models use contact force without demonstrations or finetuning. It relies on self-terminating force primitives and a layer that converts force traces into physical quantities with uncertainty. They report that, on a real arm lacking a wrist sensor, zero-shot success of GPT-6 Astra rose from 3.3% to 90.0%.

SciResearch-Eval: Evaluating Scientific Judgment in Autonomous Experimental Agents

The authors build an interactive benchmark where agents design heating experiments, fit a supplied and possibly flawed heat-conduction simulator, and report material properties with 95% intervals. Across 9 models they report that when the simulator omits physics, most models give confident but wrong values, with interval coverage of 20 to 62% against 94% and 99% for two models.

DexFormer 2.0: Towards a Cross-Embodiment Foundation Policy for Dexterous Grasping

The authors train a single reinforcement learning policy for dexterous grasping across arm and hand pairings, using a canonical joint interface with validity masks, attention biased by the kinematic tree, and Transformer-XL memory. They report 89% stable success on seen combinations, 51% on six held-out pairs, zero-shot grasping with new hands, and sim-to-real transfer on a Flexiv arm.

Jev-as-a-Reward: Validating Probabilistic Safety Judges for Reinforcement Learning

The authors propose a protocol for validating probabilistic safety judges used as reinforcement learning rewards, separating how often harm passes with high confidence from its amount and rechecking after judge updates. Applying it to a commercial judge and an open guard classifier, they report that passed harm concentrates in a confident blind spot that frontier LLM judges also mostly miss.

Benchleaking Is Accelerating: Benchmark contamination has worsened sharply in the past month -- from post-training down to pre-training base models

Right Goal, Wrong Body: Re-grounding Frozen VLAs under Actuation Shift

The authors present ReGround, which leaves a vision-language-action model frozen and, after detecting changes in robot actuation such as latency or wear, learns the new dynamics from motor babbling to steer toward the policy's intended motion. arXiv · cs.AI16 November 2026

Right Reason, Wrong Advice: Evaluating Recourse in LLM Explanations of Denied Requests

The authors define recourse validity, meaning altering only what an LLM explanation cites can reverse a denial, and find that in six of seven rule systems a single correct reason rarely yields recourse when multiple reasons apply. Across 20 LLMs, they report that checking reason correctness barely beats chance at detecting this, while asking a strong judge whether acting would succeed does detect it.
