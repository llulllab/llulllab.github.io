Concordat: Typed Sessions for LLM Agents

The authors propose Concordat, a Python library that enforces finite-state session-type protocols on LLM agents at runtime, restricting tool access to only those legal in the current protocol state and rejecting out-of-protocol calls before side effects occur. They report that on tau-bench airline tasks, the Concordat agent made zero unauthorized changes even under adversarial customer pressure, while a standard agent violated confirmation requirements in 13 to 29 of tasks depending on the model.

Do bots feel bad? Assessing empirical evidence for functional valence in LLMs

The author assesses empirical evidence for functional valence in LLMs, examining recent claims of functional pleasure, pain, and emotions. They argue that key aspects of valence as understood in philosophy and cognitive science remain untested, and that current experimental paradigms inadequately distinguish functional valence from neighboring constructs like preferences, value judgments, and role-play.

ThinkWorld: World Imagination Reinforces Chain-of-Dynamics Reasoning

The authors introduce ThinkWorld, a VLA learning framework that represents action reasoning as a Chain-of-Dynamics, a sequence of discrete motion tokens capturing intermediate dynamics between current and future states, serving as a shared action representation across embodiments. They report that reinforcement learning with world imagination enables human videos without action labels to provide direct supervision, demonstrating effective human-to-robot transfer and generalization to unseen tasks in both simulation and real robots.

Pushing Dexterity and Safety for In-Hand Object Pose Reaching That Works Remarkably Well

This paper addresses in-hand object pose reaching with a LEAP hand, training policies in simulation using diverse resets, generated grasp datasets, and an adaptive friction curriculum, then transferring them zero-shot to real hardware. A learned residual safety filter reduces drops by 75% while maintaining 91.5% goal success, with the state-based policy reaching 95.2% of goals across 4,800 unseen targets in 13.3 hours of evaluation.

Rare, Relevant, and Still Wrong: How Memory Fails LLM Group Decisions

The authors introduce CrossProfile, a benchmark of 60 templates yielding 480 tasks that disentangles fact ownership from usefulness in hidden-profile group decisions made by LLMs. Testing across multiple models reveals that both rarity-based and relevance-based memory heuristics fail predictably, and that removing or randomizing agent attribution tags raises accuracy by 13 to 24 points in classic configurations.

arXiv · cs.RO27 October 2026

When, Where, and What to Correct: Human-Guided Adaptive Residual Correction for Robot Policies

The authors propose Adaptive Residual Correction, a post-training module that applies reinforcement learning to learn residual corrections atop a frozen imitation policy, using sparse human interventions to localize when and where corrections are needed. Across three real-robot precision manipulation tasks, this approach improves average success from 33.3% to 75.0% while confining exploration to low-dimensional subspaces retaining over 96% of correction magnitude.
