MIT News·18 November 2026

Helping AI text detectors keep up with changing conditions

Researchers from MIT and Cornell University report that tools for spotting machine-written text can be misled by disguised output, by shifts in writing habits and by new language models. Their paper applies test-time adaptation, which lets a deployed detector adjust itself using unlabeled data. They say a commercial detector caught about 27 percent of disguised AI text, while their approach reached 93 percent accuracy.

EPFL·18 November 2026

Parkinson's hallucinations linked to sleep-like brain waves

EPFL scientists report a connection between slow, sleep-resembling brain waves occurring in awake Parkinson's patients and both hallucinations and thinking problems, which could help flag people likely to develop worse symptoms. The team, headed by Olaf Blanke and Fosco Bernasconi, asked whether such waves, also seen locally in alert healthy people, are stronger in the disease and tied to these effects.

The Resolution Hypothesis: Agent Evolution's Key Is Identifying Improvements

The authors argue that agent evolution loops stall because noisy evaluations cannot reliably tell better candidate prompts, skills or memories from worse ones. Across seven methods and six models, they report that selection performs near chance at the candidate level and that typical method margins fall within run-to-run variation.

Hidden-State Similarity Predicts Re-Elicitation After Inoculation Prompting

The authors study why misaligned behavior suppressed by inoculation prompting reappears under certain evaluation prompts. Worldviews in Large Language Models

The authors give two human worldview surveys to eight language models and report that the models describe the world as less good, safe and alive than humans do, a pattern weaker in base models. They also report that system prompts and model-to-model dialogue shift these worldviews and relate to safer responses.

Body Schema Policy: Body as the Vision-Action Interface for Efficient Robot Loco-Manipulation

Body Schema Policy is an imitation learning approach that arranges joint and link tokens along a robot's kinematic tree, letting each body part pull its own visual cues to guide action denoising. The authors report it equals the Ψ0 humanoid model on nine simulated Unitree G1 tasks using 75 times fewer parameters, nearly doubles Diffusion Policy's success, and also works on real hardware.

Heave-Ho: Decentralized Long-Horizon Multi-Humanoid Collaboration

Heave-Ho is a decentralized system for multiple humanoids performing long-horizon joint loco-manipulation, pairing a post-trained foundation tracking controller with a vision-language planner and spoken leader-follower synchronization that avoids network links. Its authors say training on two identical robots extends to teams of ten and mixed teams, and they present HH-Bench with 12 tasks, reporting gains over baselines.
