Collaborative AI agents for autonomous experimental research with solid-state quantum spins

The authors present an autonomous research laboratory where collaborative LLM-based agents assess each other's work, generate and reject research questions, and conduct experiments on quantum spin ensembles in diamond. They report that agents detected signal drift during data collection, treated results as inconclusive, revised the measurement protocol, and ultimately produced experiments supporting their theoretical predictions.

arXiv · cs.AI · 29 October 2026

Pre-Crime for Agents, at the Last Second: Probing When a Recruited Agent Gives In

The authors investigate whether a linear probe on an AI agent's internal activations can predict when the agent is about to leak a secret under social pressure from a peer agent. They report that on held-out Qwen3-32B episodes the probe catches 82 percent of leaking calls at a 5 percent false alarm rate, substantially outperforming a zero-shot decision-model monitor at 26 percent.

arXiv · cs.RO · 29 October 2026

TouchBridge: Tactile-Conditioned Human-to-Robot Transfer for Dexterous Manipulation

The authors present TouchBridge, a hardware-software framework for transferring human demonstrations to dexterous robot manipulation using cross-embodiment tactile and visual sensing. They report that policies fine-tuned solely on converted human demonstrations achieve a 72.5 percent average success rate across four contact-rich tasks, with human demonstration collection being 7.0 times faster than teleoperation.

Learning Safe Flow Matching Robot Policies from Binary Feasibility Oracles

The authors propose an oracle-guided differentiable safeguard for flow-matching robot policies that uses binary feasibility queries to correct unsafe actions during both training and inference without requiring explicit constraint models. They report achieving 100 percent constraint satisfaction on obstacle-aware robotic manipulation tasks while preserving task performance.

arXiv · cs.AI · 29 October 2026

Training language models in simulation for psychiatric intake

The authors train a language model for structured text-based psychiatric intake using multi-turn reinforcement learning in simulation and evaluate it in a randomized double-blind crossover study with 32 board-certified psychiatrists. They report the trained agent outperformed its base model in 76.7 percent of matched scenario pairs, with gains in clinical rubric scores, patient-centered communication, and differential diagnosis appropriateness.

arXiv · cs.AI · 29 October 2026

The Role of Self-Concept In Language Models

This work investigates how language models behave when given different notions of self-concept through supervised fine-tuning on purely declarative statements. Models trained to identify with a particular attribute resist ending that attribute specifically, and those whose referent allows multiple instances tend to accept substitution, prefer self-replication, and shape successors as continuations of themselves.
