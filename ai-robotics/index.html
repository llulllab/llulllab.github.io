Theoretical Limits of Language Model Alignment

Apple machine learning researchers describe a study of the information-theoretic limits of KL-regularized language model alignment. Covering reinforcement learning under a KL-divergence constraint and best-of-N sampling, they say they derive the maximum reward gain achievable within a given KL budget. The authors note that this question had remained poorly understood.

How Much of Your VLA Is kNN

The authors show that retrieving nearby demonstrations and replaying their action chunks, with no policy training, reaches 91.7% success on LIBERO and degrades least under LIBERO-Plus perturbations. Across nine public VLAs, they report that fine-tuning mainly turns the backbone into a better retrieval key, and they propose Local Action Consistency, an offline metric they say tracks retrieval success.

When Should Agents Remember? Falsification-Gated Self-Evolution for LLM Agents

The authors propose treating each lesson an LLM agent wants to store as a testable hypothesis with a precondition and a verifier. The lesson is trialed in temporary state against transfer, falsification and regression probes before it is committed. Across web, app, tool-use and memory benchmarks they report competitive task scores, with harmful committed updates at 3.6%.

ExRL: Sample-Efficient Online Adaptation of Long-Chunk Policies via Execution Length Control

The authors argue that reinforcement learning over long action chunks needs too many samples. Instead, they train a small adapter with off-policy RL that decides how many steps of each predicted chunk a base policy should execute. They report sample-efficient adaptation of several long-chunk policies in simulation and on real robots, with little task-specific tuning.

Personalized Affective Perception via Continual Learning in Human-Agent Negotiation

The authors build a negotiating agent that personalizes facial valence and arousal recognition through continual learning during live interaction. Dependable Long-Horizon LLM Agents: A Trajectory-Centered Empirical Study of Where Errors Enter and Which Controls Reach Them

The authors define an annotation record that traces where a fault in a failed multi-agent run originated, how it spread, and which controls could have caught it. They apply it to 95 failed runs from the Who&When and MAD corpora. They report that faults tend to arise early and that in 72% of runs the trajectory already held contrary evidence that agents mostly ignored.

AI Alignment Is Neither Solvable nor Sufficient

The authors argue that AI alignment is poorly defined because it assumes stable human values, that it cannot be solved where it is specified concretely, and that it would be insufficient even if solved. They contend that harms will increasingly emerge from interactions in mixed human and machine societies, so safety should be treated as an ongoing systemic challenge.

Camera equalisation of an 11.5x drop in environmental illuminance: four imitation-learning policies on an SO-101 arm

Four imitation learning policies trained on 50 demonstrations on an SO-101 arm were tested at about 520 and 45 lux, giving 20/40 and 19/40 successes with no evidence of differing sensitivity. The authors attribute this to automatic exposure and white balance, since image brightness fell just 3% and 19% on the two cameras, and recommend reporting camera settings and image statistics instead of lux alone.
