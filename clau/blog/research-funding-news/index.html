Research Funding News for Doctoral and Early-Career Researchers

Compiled by the Clau team. As of 30 October 2026.

This digest gathers a selection of notable announcements from the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions programme that are relevant to doctoral candidates and early-career researchers, spanning funding increases, anniversary milestones, Nobel Prize connections, and new resources.

These summaries are written by Clau and each links to its original announcement. Clau is independent and not affiliated with the European Union or its research bodies.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions · 21 October 2026

Interactive publication featuring over 100 success stories and testimonials

The Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions has published an interactive collection of over 100 success stories and testimonials from funded researchers. Readers can search by country or institution, making it a useful resource for doctoral candidates and postdocs who want to see concrete examples of how the programme has supported research careers across Europe.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions · 20 October 2026

Science without borders: the lasting legacy of Marie Skłodowska-Curie

An article in Horizon Magazine traces the legacy of the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions, which in 2026 marks its 30th anniversary. Since 1996, the programme has supported more than 150,000 researchers, including 25 Nobel laureates, and has grown from roughly 800 participants per year to around 8,000. Companies, including a growing share of small and medium-sized enterprises, account for nearly half of participating organisations, underscoring the programme's role in connecting early-career researchers with industry.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions · 6 October 2026

Nobel Prizes in Medicine and Physics awarded to former MSCA fellow and supervisor

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine was awarded jointly to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann, and Georg Nagel for discoveries in optogenetics; Nagel was a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions fellow at the University of Naples Federico II from 1999 to 2000. The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics went to Francis Halzen for contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory; Halzen served as a supervisor in the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions funded ASTRONU project on neutrino astronomy. These awards illustrate how early and mid-career support through the programme can contribute to research of the highest impact.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions · 30 September 2026

Over €210 million boost for the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions to support more researchers in Europe

The European Commission has announced an additional 210 million euro top-up for the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions, directed at Postdoctoral Fellowships and Doctoral Networks. The boost responds to record demand: the 2026 Postdoctoral Fellowships call attracted 21,627 applications, the highest number ever recorded for any single funding action across more than 40 years of EU research framework programmes. With over 1.3 billion euro foreseen for the programme in 2027, this increase is significant for early-career researchers seeking funding under the final calls of Horizon Europe.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions · 25 September 2026

Cancer research in the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions

The Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions has released a document highlighting cancer research funded through the programme under Horizon Europe (2021 to 2027), which has a total budget of 6.6 billion euro. The programme funds bottom-up, researcher-driven projects in all fields based on scientific excellence, so early-career researchers working in oncology or related disciplines may find this resource helpful for understanding how their work fits within the programme's scope.
