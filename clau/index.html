Doctoral funding radar · 13 November 2026

Doctoral and Early-Career Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

Planning a next funding step works best when you look beyond the nearest deadline and think about where you want your research to be in a few years. The calls below cover doctoral training, postdoctoral career building, and grants for researchers consolidating a research programme of their own, under the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions and the European Research Council. Each entry sets out what the call funds and one practical thing you can do ahead of time.

Of the five calls below, three are applied for by institutions or consortia, with individual positions recruited after the grant is awarded, and two are applied for directly by the individual researcher, together with a host organisation.

ERC Consolidator Grant

ERC Consolidator Grant

Open Closes on 12 January 2027 60 days left

Who applies. The individual researcher applies directly, together with a chosen host organisation.

The Consolidator Grant supports Principal Investigators who are consolidating their own independent research team or programme. It funds a sustained, multi-year research agenda led by you, so the proposal is about the scientific direction you set and the group you build around it. The description also allows a justified request for additional funding when specific costs are necessary for the proposed work.

Preparation step. Work out which parts of your programme would need resources beyond a standard budget, such as equipment or access to facilities, and write a short justification for each, because any request for additional funding must be duly justified in the proposal. Before investing more time, verify all eligibility conditions on the official topic page and start a conversation with your prospective host organisation.

Official topic page: ERC-2027-COG

MSCA COFUND (doctoral programmes)

MSCA COFUND 2027

Opens on 8 December 2026 Closes on 6 April 2027 144 days left

Who applies. Institutions or consortia apply. Individual positions are recruited openly after the grant is awarded.

This Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions COFUND call supports organisations that run doctoral programmes, so the proposal comes from the organisation rather than from candidates. For a prospective doctoral student, its value lies in the positions these programmes open once they are funded, typically with an emphasis on interdisciplinary and international experience and on communicating research to peers and the public. Quality and sustainability of research training are explicit aims for the participating organisations.

Preparation step

Make a list of graduate schools and universities in your field that run doctoral programmes co-funded through the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions, follow their vacancy pages and mailing lists, and watch for the doctoral positions they advertise after an award. While you wait, build evidence of interdisciplinary work and public engagement that a selection panel for such a programme would value.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2027-COFUND-01-01

MSCA COFUND (postdoctoral programmes)

MSCA Choose Europe for Science 2027

Opens on 8 December 2026 Closes on 6 April 2027 144 days left

Who applies. Institutions or consortia apply. Individual positions are recruited openly after the grant is awarded.

Choose Europe for Science is a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions COFUND strand for postdoctoral programmes, and organisations put the proposal forward. Researchers recruited into the funded programmes later can expect a focus on concrete career prospects, greater career stability, and growing autonomy. The call also asks participating organisations to align their recruitment and career progression practices with the European Charter for Researchers and the Agreement on Reforming Research Assessment.

Preparation step

Identify universities and research centres that publicly commit to the European Charter for Researchers and to research assessment reform, then monitor them for postdoctoral openings under this scheme once programmes are funded. In parallel, prepare a narrative CV that presents your research, teaching and wider contributions in the broader terms that reformed assessment practices tend to reward.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2027-COFUND-02-01

MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships

MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships 2027

Opens on 7 April 2027 Closes on 8 September 2027 299 days left

Who applies. The individual researcher applies directly, together with a chosen host organisation.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Postdoctoral Fellowships fund a researcher to carry out their own project at a host organisation, so you shape the research question and the training plan yourself. Expected results include broader research and professional competences, interdisciplinary and inter-sectoral experience, and stronger links with scientific peers and the public. Host organisations, for their part, are expected to provide good supervision and working conditions in line with the European Charter for Researchers.

Preparation step

Draft a one-page project outline, then approach a prospective supervisor at a host organisation early so that the training and supervision plan can be developed together rather than added at the last minute. Before going further, check the mobility rule and every other eligibility condition on the official topic page.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2027-PF-01-01

MSCA Doctoral Networks

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2027

Opens on 26 May 2027 Closes on 23 November 2027 375 days left

Who applies. Institutions or consortia apply. Individual positions are recruited openly after the grant is awarded.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks are proposed by groups of organisations that design joint doctoral training, and candidates join the funded projects only after award. Doctoral candidates in these networks gain new research and professional skills, opportunities to turn ideas into products and services where relevant, and connections that reach across sectors and disciplines. The participating organisations commit to doctoral training and supervision that is of high quality, relevant and sustainable.

Preparation step

Track the research groups and companies in your field that already take part in doctoral networks, for example through project websites and network announcements, and set alerts for the doctoral positions they post after funding. A short internship, a placement in industry or a collaboration with a non-academic partner will strengthen your profile for these training-oriented positions.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2027-DN-01-01

European Research Council · 12 November 2026

Synergy Grants 2026: Examples of projects

The European Research Council presents examples of projects funded in its 2026 collaborative team grants, including NAVAR, which studies how childhood languages change when bilingual speakers raised in Germany with Polish or Portuguese later relocate to Poland or Portugal. The team plans to track first-language change over the lifespan and to build an open-access database. Researchers in linguistics, migration studies or cognition may find a future data resource here, as well as a model of how a cross-institutional project is framed for top-tier funding.

European Research Council · 12 November 2026

ERC Synergy Grants 2026: Bringing 48 research teams together to tackle science’s biggest challenges

The European Research Council has announced the results of its 2026 team-based grant call, funding 48 groups of two to four researchers who combine complementary expertise to address questions none could tackle alone. According to the Commissioner, 17 of the selected teams include researchers based outside Europe. For early-career researchers, the scheme shows how ambitious, curiosity-driven collaboration is supported under Horizon Europe and how international partnerships can feature in such proposals.

Read the full announcement at European Research Council

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions · 11 November 2026

Developing talents, advancing research : 30 years of EU support to researchers’ skills and career development

This document marks 30 years of EU support for researcher training and career development through the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions, which hold a budget of €6.6 billion for 2021 to 2027. The programme funds bottom-up research in all fields and at every career stage, with an emphasis on skills, career progression and international mobility, and it has supported more than 150,000 researchers, including 25 Nobel laureates. It offers useful context for doctoral and postdoctoral researchers considering these fellowships and networks.

Read the full announcement at Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions

European Research Council · 5 November 2026

Commission invites nominations for the next President of the European Research Council

The European Commission is inviting research organisations across the EU to put forward candidates for the next President of the European Research Council, with nominations due in late November 2026. The successor to Professor Maria Leptin will begin in autumn 2027 for a four-year term that can be renewed once. Although not a funding opportunity, the appointment matters to early-career researchers because the President helps steer one of Europe's main sources of frontier research funding.

European Research Council · 23 October 2026

Starting Grants 2027 applications: Facts and figures

Preliminary figures for the ERC Starting Grants 2027 call, which closed in October 2026, show 7 710 applications, a rise of 60.4% over the previous call and following earlier increases of 22.4% and 13%. Physical Sciences and Engineering accounted for 43% of proposals, Social Sciences and Humanities for 29% and Life Sciences for 28%, while women submitted nearly 39%. Early-career researchers planning a Starting Grant application should expect a markedly more crowded competition.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions · 21 October 2026

Interactive publication featuring over 100 success stories and testimonials

The Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions have released an interactive publication bringing together more than 100 success stories and testimonials. Readers can browse by country or by host institution to see how funded researchers and projects have contributed to Europe. Prospective applicants may find it a practical way to discover funded work at their own university and to learn how others have shaped their careers through the programme.
