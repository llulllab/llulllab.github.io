Doctoral funding radar · 20 November 2026

Doctoral and Early-Career Research Funding Calls Worth Preparing For

These calls trace a research career from structured doctoral training, through postdoctoral fellowships and programmes, to grants that consolidate an independent research agenda. Read them as a map rather than a menu: knowing where each instrument sits helps you decide what to prepare for next and what to watch from a distance. Each note below sets out what the call funds and one practical move that follows from it.

Of the five calls below, three are applied for by institutions or consortia, with individual positions recruited after the grant is awarded, and two are applied for directly by the individual researcher, together with a host organisation.

ERC Consolidator Grant

ERC Consolidator Grant

Open Closes on 12 January 2027 53 days left

Who applies. The individual researcher applies directly, together with a chosen host organisation.

The Consolidator Grant funds Principal Investigators who are consolidating their own independent research team or programme, so the proposal centres on a research vision you already own and intend to expand. The call text stresses evidence of research independence, which means your track record has to show leadership of your own agenda, not only strong output. Additional funding can be requested for further costs where these are necessary to carry out the work, provided the request is properly justified in the proposal.

Preparation step. Sketch the team or programme you intend to consolidate and list the concrete evidence of independence you can point to, such as lines of work you lead, people you supervise, or publications shaped by your own questions. If you anticipate costs beyond the standard grant, write down early why they are necessary, since any request for additional funding must be justified in the proposal. Before investing serious time, verify all eligibility conditions on the official topic page.

MSCA COFUND (doctoral programmes)

MSCA COFUND 2027

Opens on 8 December 2026 Closes on 6 April 2027 137 days left

Who applies. Institutions or consortia apply. Individual positions are recruited openly after the grant is awarded.

This Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions COFUND call is an opportunity for organisations to co-finance doctoral programmes; individual candidates do not take part in this round's proposal. If funded, those programmes will later open doctoral positions designed to widen research and professional competences and to offer international, inter-sectoral and interdisciplinary exposure. For prospective doctoral candidates, the value lies in the programmes this call may create and in the training quality and visibility the participating organisations are expected to build.

Preparation step

Identify universities and graduate schools in your field that run or are developing co-funded doctoral programmes under the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions, and follow their news and vacancy pages so you notice the doctoral positions they advertise once a grant is awarded, often well after this deadline. In the meantime, build evidence of communicating your research to non-specialist audiences, since engagement with the general public is among the outcomes these programmes aim for.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2027-COFUND-01-01

MSCA COFUND (postdoctoral programmes)

MSCA Choose Europe for Science 2027

Opens on 8 December 2026 Closes on 6 April 2027 137 days left

Who applies. Institutions or consortia apply. Individual positions are recruited openly after the grant is awarded.

Choose Europe for Science is a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions COFUND call through which organisations seek co-funding for postdoctoral programmes, so researchers are not part of the proposal at this stage. The programmes it may fund aim to give postdoctoral researchers concrete career prospects, greater career stability and growing autonomy. Participating organisations are also expected to align their recruitment and career progression practices with the European Charter for Researchers and the Agreement on Reforming Research Assessment, which shapes how future positions will be assessed.

Preparation step

Make a shortlist of research organisations in your area that are likely to run co-funded postdoctoral programmes, and keep an eye on their career pages for the positions they will open later if funded. Use the waiting time to revise your CV so it presents a broad range of contributions (teaching, supervision, collaboration, outputs beyond articles), in line with the research assessment reform these programmes are expected to adopt.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2027-COFUND-02-01

MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships

MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships 2027

Opens on 7 April 2027 Closes on 8 September 2027 292 days left

Who applies. The individual researcher applies directly, together with a chosen host organisation.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Postdoctoral Fellowships fund a researcher's own project carried out together with a host organisation, so you design the research and its training component yourself. The expected outcomes include a larger set of research and professional competences, stronger prospects for a career inside or outside universities, and fresh approaches shaped by international, inter-sectoral and interdisciplinary work. Host organisations are expected to provide quality training and supervision and working conditions consistent with the European Charter for Researchers, which gives you concrete criteria for choosing where to go.

Preparation step

Draft a one-page outline of the project and the skills you want to gain, then contact a prospective supervisor at a host organisation early to discuss how their training and supervision arrangements would support that plan. Before going further, check the mobility rule and every other eligibility condition on the official topic page.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2027-PF-01-01

MSCA Doctoral Networks

MSCA Doctoral Networks 2027

Opens on 26 May 2027 Closes on 23 November 2027 368 days left

Who applies. Institutions or consortia apply. Individual positions are recruited openly after the grant is awarded.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions Doctoral Networks are proposed by groups of organisations that design joint doctoral training; candidates are hired only after a network is funded, through separate vacancy calls. The resulting positions aim to develop new research and professional competences, broaden career options, and, where relevant, help turn ideas into products and services. Because the networks emphasise cooperation and knowledge transfer between sectors and disciplines, they suit candidates who want doctoral work that connects academic research with other settings.

Preparation step

Follow research groups and non-academic partners in your field that take part in Doctoral Networks, for example by subscribing to their newsletters or job alerts, so you can respond quickly when funded networks advertise their doctoral positions, often a year or more after this deadline. Meanwhile, look for a short placement, project or collaboration outside academia that shows you can work across sectors.

Official topic page: HORIZON-MSCA-2027-DN-01-01

European Research Council · 12 November 2026

Synergy Grants 2026: Examples of projects

The European Research Council highlights NAVAR, one of the team projects funded in its 2026 round, which examines how migration affects the languages people learned in early childhood. The team will study bilingual speakers raised in Germany with Polish or Portuguese as a heritage language who later relocated to Poland or Portugal, tracking how first languages change over the lifespan and building an open-access database. For early-career linguists and cognitive scientists, the project offers an example of the collaborative, cross-institutional research the ERC supports and a data resource that may prove useful for future work.

European Research Council · 12 November 2026

ERC Synergy Grants 2026: Bringing 48 research teams together to tackle science’s biggest challenges

The European Research Council has announced funding for 48 teams in its 2026 round of grants for groups of two to four researchers with complementary expertise, part of Horizon Europe. According to the announcement, 17 of the selected teams include researchers based outside Europe, and the scheme leaves the choice of research questions and methods to the applicants. Early-career researchers may find these funded teams a source of possible positions, and the scheme a model of how international collaboration can be structured in frontier research.

Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions · 11 November 2026

Developing talents, advancing research : 30 years of EU support to researchers’ skills and career development

A new publication from the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions reviews three decades of EU investment in the skills and career development of researchers. It describes the programme as the EU's main research and training scheme, with a budget of €6.6 billion for 2021 to 2027, open to all disciplines and career stages, and credits it with supporting more than 150,000 researchers, among them 25 Nobel laureates. For doctoral and postdoctoral researchers, it offers useful context on a central funding route for training, mobility and career progression.

European Research Council · 5 November 2026

Commission invites nominations for the next President of the European Research Council

The European Commission is inviting research organisations across the EU to nominate candidates for the next President of the European Research Council, with nominations due in late November 2026. The appointee will succeed Professor Maria Leptin in autumn 2027 for a four-year term that may be renewed once. Although early-career researchers will not take part in the nomination directly, the leadership of the ERC shapes one of the most important funding bodies for researchers in Europe.

European Research Council · 23 October 2026

Starting Grants 2027 applications: Facts and figures

Preliminary figures for the ERC Starting Grants 2027 call, which closed in October 2026, show 7,710 applications, a rise of 60.4% on the previous call, following earlier increases of 22.4% and 13% in the two calls before it. Physical Sciences and Engineering accounted for 43% of proposals, Social Sciences and Humanities for 29% and Life Sciences for 28%, while women submitted close to 39% of applications. For early-career researchers planning to apply, these numbers point to sharply increasing competition for this key grant.
