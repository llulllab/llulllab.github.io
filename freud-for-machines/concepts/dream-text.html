The dream text (der Traumtext)

In Freud's texts. Entry · Long entry · All entries · Ask the research tool

In brief

In the passages read, this family of terms carries four senses.

  1. The told dream as the material of interpretation. Freud weighs its wording "like a sacred text": «das haben wir behandelt wie einen heiligen Text» [GW II/III:518]. This sense runs from 1900 to 1933.
  2. The wording of a verbal element inside the dream itself. This sense occurs once, in 1900 [GW II/III:323], with a related use in 1901. Freud says only that the dream-work failed to make one wording for the dream thoughts. That the two versions are therefore its product is an inference [ii]. The page leaves open whether the two versions belong to the dream or to the teller's description, so separating this sense from Sense 1 is an interpretation [ii].
  3. The Traumtext as an object of preservation. This is the wording of the remembered or told dream, at issue in treatment technique when Freud asks whether it should be kept or fixed. He judges the effort to preserve it therapeutically superfluous (1911 to 1933) [GW VIII:356]. Writing the dream down is a practice applied to this text, not the text itself. Counting it apart from Senses 1 and 4 is an interpretation [ii].
  4. "Traumtext" as a technical name for the manifest dream, set against the latent dream thoughts: «den Traumtext oder den manifesten Traum» [GW XV:9]. The name is given in 1933. In the pages read, the manifest/latent pair it joins appears from 1913 onward [GW X:17].

The main dated line is this. In the passages read, the noun "Traumtext" first appears in 1911, for the dream text whose faithful preservation is at issue: «die getreue Erhaltung des Traumtextes» [GW VIII:355]. It becomes a name of the manifest dream only in 1933. All dates come from the source labels unless a page prints them.

The distinction a reader must not miss concerns the dream as dreamt versus the dream as told. In 1900 Freud concedes that the told dream is distorted in reproduction [GW II/III:518]. In 1933 "what we heard" is named the manifest dream [GW XV:10]. The same 1933 pages still read a piece added later to the telling as an attempt to forget: «Das ist als ein Versuch aufzufassen, dieses Stück zu vergessen.» [GW XV:14]

Entry

In the passages read, this family of terms carries four senses, and the count is itself an interpretation; all dates come from the source labels unless a page prints them. On our reading, Senses 1, 3 and 4 share one kind of object, the wording of the remembered or told dream, and differ in role (material for interpretation, something to be kept or lost, a defined term): Sense 4 renames what was heard, the object of Sense 1, as the manifest dream (GW XV:10), and in 1933 one sentence identifies the preserved text with the manifest dream (GW XV:14), though in 1911 and 1933 Sense 3 concerns the dream as remembered on waking rather than always the told dream. Sense 2, the wording of a placard read inside the dream, has a referent of its own only on one reading of GW II/III:322-323, a layered page whose footnote cites a 1910 publication; on the other reading it is a case of Sense 1, the pages do not show whether this use is Freud's own, though they credit Delboeuf with recognising the origin of dream speeches (GW II/III:190), it is not found after 1901 in the passages read, and the texts read do not say whether it is abandoned or kept. For Sense 1, Freud claims as his own in 1900 the treatment of the told dream "like a sacred text", against the authors who took the report for a hasty improvisation and against Spitta (GW II/III:517-518); he says his piece-by-piece method approaches the cipher method as an interpretation in detail but has no fixed key (GW II/III:108-109), the pages leave open whether he also takes over its view of the dream as a secret script, and a footnote on Artemidoros says his technique departs from the ancient one by laying the work of interpretation on the dreamer (GW II/III:102); the pages do not date that footnote's layer, but its continuation refers to a 1913 publication, so on our reading it reaches at least to 1913. The practice of Sense 1 runs from 1900 through Ein Traum als Beweismittel (1913, the telling with its circumstances, GW X:16) and the Vorlesungen (1916, GW XI:112, 116) to 1933 (GW XV:10, 14), but the "sacred text" formula does not recur in the passages read, and the weight of the wording is held in tension at each date: in 1900 every nuance counts yet recovering the dream "does not matter", in 1916 fidelity of recall is indifferent yet nothing in the dream is accidental, and in 1933 the manifest dream gets as little attention as possible yet much of it is promised as usable; one licensed reading is that Freud sets the apparent sense aside while attending to every element as a path, but he does not state this, and the tension stays. The noun "Traumtext" first appears in the pages read in 1911, in Freud's report of others' care for preserving it (GW VIII:355), and the pages do not show whether it is his coinage; he judges the measure of writing each dream down immediately on waking superfluous for therapy while granting that the doctor learns something (GW VIII:356), keeps that judgment in 1933 by calling it useless (GW XV:14), and in 1912 has dream texts that matter to him fixed after the telling (GW VIII:379), which fits the 1911 verdict once the different time point and purpose are stated, while what stays in tension is this therapeutic indifference against his care for printed wording, where he declares his one substitution (GW II/III:369, probably a post-1900 layer on our reading). The manifest/latent pair appears from 1913 in the pages read (GW X:17) and is named as terms in 1916 (GW XI:118), but only in 1933 does Freud name the Traumtext as the manifest dream, set against the latent dream thoughts (GW XV:9), with a "wir" that makes the use his own only by inference; the same lecture both treats the distinction as already in place (GW XV:7) and calls its terms new, a tension left standing, and since it is the last text read, the texts read do not say whether Sense 4 is kept or revised. The distinction a reader must not miss is the dream as dreamt versus the dream as told: in 1900 Freud concedes that reproduction distorts the dream (GW II/III:518), and in 1933 the naming equates what was heard with the manifest dream while the same pages read a later addendum as an attempt to forget, so on our reading these pages assume a gap the naming does not mark; nor should Sense 1 be confused with speech inside the dream or the dream façade, Sense 2 with the "Text" of the dream thoughts that the dream works over (GW II/III:319), Sense 3 with the act of writing or the exact protocols Freud dismisses as sham exactness (GW VIII:379), or Sense 4 with the façade, the 1900 use of "manifest" for the distortion in reproduction (GW II/III:519), or the 1900 "latent Trauminhalt" (GW II/III:517).

Senses. 1. The told dream as the material of interpretation, 1900 to 1933 (Die Traumdeutung 1900; Ein Traum als Beweismittel 1913; Vorlesungen 1916, where the one occurrence of "Traumtext" (GW XI:116) is counted with this sense on our reading, not with Sense 3; Neue Folge 1933): the wording of the reported dream, used element by element as evidence. 2. Wording inside the dream, 1900 (Die Traumdeutung VI.C, a layered page), with a related use on dream speeches in Über den Traum, 1901 (GW II/III:681): the wording of a verbal element within the dream itself, a separate sense only on one reading. 3. The Traumtext as an object of preservation, 1911 (Handhabung der Traumdeutung), 1912 (Ratschläge für den Arzt) and 1933 (Neue Folge XXIX): the wording of the remembered or told dream as something to be kept, fixed or lost, counted apart on our reading. 4. Name for the manifest dream, 1933 (Neue Folge XXIX), with the manifest/latent pair present from 1913 (Ein Traum als Beweismittel) and named as terms in 1916 (Vorlesungen) in the pages read: "Traumtext" as a technical name for the manifest dream, set against the latent dream thoughts.

Where to read. - GW II/III:318-325 (Die Traumdeutung, VI.C, 1900) - GW II/III:516-535 (Die Traumdeutung, VII.A, 1900) - GW VIII:350-357 (Handhabung der Traumdeutung, 1911) - GW VIII:376-382 (Ratschläge für den Arzt, 1912) - GW XI:111-124 (Vorlesungen, VII, 1916) - GW XV:6-16 (Neue Folge, XXIX, 1933)

Long entry

The senses

I count four senses, and the count is an interpretation [ii]. Only Sense 2 may have a referent of its own, and only on one reading of GW II/III:322-323: wording inside the dream rather than the teller's wording (see Sense 2). Senses 1, 3 and 4 have the same kind of referent, namely the wording of the remembered or told dream. They differ in role or status. In Sense 1 that object is material for interpretation. In Sense 3 it is something to be kept or lost and judged by its therapeutic worth. In Sense 4 it is a defined term in the manifest/latent pair: «Wir heißen, was man den Traum genannt hat, den Traumtext oder den manifesten Traum» [GW XV:9]. Separating these roles into senses is an interpretation [ii]. That includes the separation of Sense 1 from Sense 4 (see How the senses relate). A reader may instead count Sense 3, or Sense 4, as an aspect of Sense 1.

Sense 1: The told dream as the material of interpretation (1900–1933)

In Freud's words. - «Die gleiche Würdigung haben wir bei der Traumdeutung jeder Nuance des sprachlichen Ausdrucks geschenkt, in welchem der Traum uns vorlag» [GW II/III:518] (In interpreting dreams we have given the same regard to every nuance of the linguistic expression in which the dream lay before us.) - «Kurz, was nach der Meinung der Autoren eine willkürliche, in der Verlegenheit eilig zusammengebraute Improvisation sein soll, das haben wir behandelt wie einen heiligen Text.» [GW II/III:518] (In short, what the authors take to be an arbitrary improvisation, hastily brewed up in embarrassment, we have treated like a sacred text.) - «kommen für die Übersetzung des Traumes nicht allein die Ergebnisse der Assoziation in Betracht, sondern auch die Begleitumstände der Traumerzählung, das Benehmen des Träumers vor und nach der Traumanalyse» [GW X:16] (For translating the dream, not only the results of association count, but also the circumstances of the dream's telling and the dreamer's conduct before and after the analysis.)

What it means. The dream reaches the analyst as something told. In one case Freud asks for a fuller telling: «ich lasse ihn mir detailliert erzählen. Dann lautet der Bericht folgendermaßen» [GW II/III:189] (I have it told to me in detail; the report then runs as follows). Interpretation takes the told dream in pieces: «Ich muß ihm den Traum zerstückt vorlegen, dann liefert er mir zu jedem Stück eine Reihe von Einfällen» [GW II/III:108] (I must lay the dream before him cut into pieces; then he gives me a series of associations to each piece).

Features of the telling count as evidence: - Insertions show themselves because they «werden zaghaft berichtet, mit einem „als ob“ eingeleitet» [GW II/III:493] (are reported hesitantly, introduced with an "as if"). - The dreamer's remarks about the dream conceal and betray at once [GW II/III:337]. - Changes between two tellings mark weak spots in the disguise [GW II/III:519]. - In one case of 1913 Freud says he would have advised beginning interpretation at a later addendum, because it was «ein Nachtrag, und zwar von besonderer Deutlichkeit» (an addendum, and one of special distinctness) [GW X:15]. The statement is about that case and depends on the addendum's distinctness.

Freud concedes that «wir den Traum beim Versuch der Reproduktion entstellen» [GW II/III:518] (we distort the dream in trying to reproduce it). He then folds this distortion into lawful revision: «Aber diese Entstellung ist selbst nichts anderes als ein Stück der Bearbeitung, welcher die Traumgedanken gesetzmäßig infolge der Traumzensur unterliegen.» [GW II/III:519] (But this distortion is itself nothing other than a piece of the working-over to which the dream thoughts are lawfully subject because of the dream censorship.) The aim of interpretation is not the dream itself: «zwar nicht den Traum — aber an dem liegt ja auch nichts — doch die Traumgedanken alle auffinden» [GW II/III:522] (one can find, not the dream, which does not matter anyway, but all the dream thoughts). The wording is therefore valued as a path to the dream thoughts, not as an end [ii].

When and where. The opening method chapter of 1900 does not use "Traumtext", "Wortlaut" or "Traumerzählung" for the material. It speaks of "Trauminhalt" [GW II/III:101, 109], "Traumelement" [GW II/III:103], "Traumgebilde" [GW II/III:106] and "Traumpartie" [GW II/III:108], and the told dream appears through the verb "erzählen" [GW II/III:105, 109]. A footnote printed on these pages ties the dream to its wording: «Übrigens hängt der Traum so innig am sprachlichen Ausdruck, daß Ferenczi mit Recht bemerken kann, jede Sprache habe ihre eigene Traumsprache.» [GW II/III:104] (The dream, moreover, hangs so closely on linguistic expression that Ferenczi can rightly remark that every language has its own dream language.) The footnotes on these pages refer to a 1913 publication and to translations of the book, so this footnote belongs to a later layer than the main text [ii]. The harmless-dream analyses of 1900 trace dream speeches word for word: «Man kann bei der Deutungsarbeit von solchen Reden ausgehen.» [GW II/III:190] (In the work of interpretation one can start from such speeches.) Chapter VII.A gives the theory of reproduction and doubt [GW II/III:516-526].

The 1900 volume is layered. Its pages print later footnotes, and the "Wortlaut des Traumes" sentence at GW II/III:369 recurs verbatim at GW XI:198 (1916). That extract's pages cite the 1916/17 lectures, so the sentence probably belongs to a post-1900 layer [ii]. It reads: «Aus Gründen der Diskretion habe ich das „Blech" für das andere Material, mit dem der Vater handelt, eingesetzt, ohne sonst etwas am Wortlaut des Traumes zu ändern.» [GW II/III:369] (For reasons of discretion I have put "sheet metal" in place of the other material the father deals in, without otherwise changing anything in the wording of the dream.)

In 1913 the noun "Traumerzählung" names the event of telling, with its circumstances [GW X:16]. In 1916 the remembered dream is «nicht das Eigentliche, sondern ein entstellter Ersatz dafür» [GW XI:112] (not the real thing but a distorted substitute for it). In the same lecture the word "Traumtext" occurs once, for the dream that is written down and set against the written associations: «so können diese leicht ein Vielfaches des Traumtextes ausmachen» [GW XI:116] (these can easily come to many times the dream text). The occurrence stands inside the work of interpretation, so I count it with this sense and not with Sense 3 [ii]. In 1933 the order of telling still matters: «Wir können einfach der chronologischen Ordnung folgen, wie sie sich bei der Erzählung des Traums herausgestellt hat. Das ist die sozusagen strengste, klassische Methode.» [GW XV:10] (We can simply follow the chronological order in which the parts came out in the telling of the dream; that is, so to speak, the strictest, classical method.) Also in 1933, an addendum is read as an attempt to forget [GW XV:14].

What stays across these texts is the element-by-element procedure and the use of features of the telling as evidence. What changes is the weight given to fidelity (see Tensions).

Freud's own or taken over. Freud does not present the piece-by-piece procedure as wholly his own. In the main text he says his method «nähert sich der zweiten, der „Chiffriermethode“» (approaches the second, the "cipher method") and «Sie ist wie diese eine Deutung en detail, nicht en masse» (like it, it is an interpretation in detail, not en masse) [GW II/III:108]. He marks his difference in two places. The first stands in a footnote on Artemidoros that begins on GW II/III:102 and continues on GW II/III:103, where it refers to a 1913 publication. There his technique «weicht von der antiken in dem einen wesentlichen Punkte ab, daß sie dem Träumer selbst die Deutungsarbeit auferlegt» [GW II/III:102] (departs from the ancient one in the one essential point that it lays the work of interpretation on the dreamer himself). The pages do not date this footnote's layer, and its continuation reaches at least to 1913 [ii]. The second difference stands in the main text: his method has no fixed key, unlike the cipher method, «welche den gegebenen Trauminhalt nach einem fixierten Schlüssel übersetzt» [GW II/III:109] (which translates the given dream content by a fixed key). In the main text of the 1900 pages read, only this second difference and the en-detail alignment [GW II/III:108] are stated. These pages leave open whether he also takes over the cipher method's view of the dream as a "Geheimschrift" (secret script) [GW II/III:102] [ii]. What he claims as his own is the regard for every detail of the report, the "sacred text" treatment. He sets this against "die Autoren" (the authors), for whom the report is an arbitrary improvisation [GW II/III:518], and against Spitta's view that coherence is first brought in at recall [GW II/III:517]. The pages name no source for the "sacred text" simile.

Abandoned or kept. The practice is kept through 1933 [GW XV:10, 14]. The "heiliger Text" formula does not recur in the passages read. The 1916 rule to set the apparent sense aside, «Man kümmere sich nicht um das, was der Traum zu besagen scheint, sei er verständig oder absurd, klar oder verworren» [GW XI:112] (One should not concern oneself with what the dream seems to say, whether it is sensible or absurd, clear or confused), restates a point already present in 1900 and 1901. In 1900 the apparent sense of a coherent, revised dream «ist von der wirklichen Bedeutung des Traums auch am weitesten entfernt» [GW II/III:494] (is also furthest from the real meaning of the dream). In 1901, on beginning the analysis, «müssen wir uns zuerst von diesem Deutungsversuche frei machen» [GW II/III:679] (we must first free ourselves from this attempt at interpretation).

Not to be confused with. Sense 1 is not the speech inside the dream, which is what is «gesagt oder gehört wird, nicht bloß gedacht» [GW II/III:190] (said or heard, not merely thought). Nor is it the "Traumfassade", the surface sense of a dream sentence: «In der Traumfassade müßte man natürlich an die Teile des überfahrenen und zermalmten Töchterchens denken. Der Einfall weist aber nach ganz anderer Richtung.» [GW II/III:367] (In the dream façade one would naturally think of the parts of the little daughter who was run over and crushed. But the association points in quite another direction.)

Sense 2: Wording inside the dream (1900–1901)

In Freud's words. - «Daher bittet der eine Wortlaut des Traumes, „ein Auge zuzudrücken", d. h. Nachsicht zu üben.» [GW II/III:323] (Hence the one wording of the dream asks to "turn a blind eye", that is, to show indulgence.) - «Es ist der Traumarbeit nicht gelungen, einen einheitlichen, aber dann zweideutigen Wortlaut für die Traumgedanken herzustellen. So sondern sich die beiden Hauptgedankenzüge schon im Trauminhalt voneinander.» [GW II/III:323] (The dream-work did not manage to produce a single, though then ambiguous, wording for the dream thoughts. So the two main trains of thought already separate in the dream content.)

What it means. Here "Wortlaut des Traumes" is the wording of a placard that was read in the dream. Freud says that the dream-work failed to produce a single wording "for the dream thoughts", and he places the separation of the two trains of thought "in the dream content". That the two versions are the dream-work's product is an inference from this failure clause, not a statement of the page [ii]. Both versions are kept: «Jede der beiden Fassungen hat ihren besonderen Sinn und führt in der Traumdeutung auf besondere Wege.» [GW II/III:323] (Each of the two versions has its own sense and leads interpretation along its own paths.)

The same pages give a related rule about dreamed speech. Spoken words in dreams are «unveränderte oder nur wenig modifizierte Nachbildungen von Reden» [GW II/III:318] (unchanged or only slightly modified copies of speeches) taken from memory. In 1901, dream speeches copy their sources' wording «aufs getreueste [...] während sie deren Veranlassung ganz beiseite lassen und ihren Sinn aufs gewaltsamste verändern» [GW II/III:681] (with the greatest fidelity, while they leave their occasion entirely aside and alter their sense most violently).

When and where. This sense occurs once, in 1900. The phrase "Wortlaut des Traumes" also occurs elsewhere in the pages read [GW II/III:369; XI:198, 207], but in other senses. A footnote on the same page cites a 1910 publication, so the page is layered. The 1901 use of "Wortlaut" concerns source speeches. The texts read show no later occurrence of this sense.

Freud's own or taken over. The pages do not show whether this use is Freud's own. They credit Delboeuf with recognising the origin of dream speeches [GW II/III:190].

Abandoned or kept. The texts read do not say.

Not to be confused with. Separating Sense 2 from the teller's wording is an interpretation, not a statement of the text [ii]. The page supports two readings. - On the first reading, the two placard versions are a separation within the dream content, as in the sentence above: «So sondern sich die beiden Hauptgedankenzüge schon im Trauminhalt voneinander.» [GW II/III:323] - On the second reading, the versions are the teller's either-or. The same page speaks of the vagueness «die wir mit einem Entweder - Oder beschrieben» [GW II/III:323] (which we described with an either-or). The previous page assigns the either-or to the report: «Wo aber der Erzähler bei der Reproduktion des Traumes ein Entweder - Oder gebrauchen möchte» [GW II/III:322] (But where the narrator, in reproducing the dream, would like to use an either-or), and «Mit Entweder - Oder beschreiben wir zumeist einen noch auflösbaren Charakter von Verschwommenheit an einem Traumelemente.» [GW II/III:322] (With either-or we mostly describe a still resolvable vagueness in a dream element.)

I keep Sense 2 as a separate sense only on two grounds, both interpretive [ii]. First, the failure clause implies that this wording falls to the dream-work, and Freud places the separation in the dream content. Second, the rule on dreamed speech also concerns wording inside the dream [GW II/III:318, 681]. If the second reading is right, the placard case is a case of Sense 1.

Sense 2 is also not the "Text" of the dream thoughts that the dream processes: «Der Traum entfernt sich hierin mehr oder weniger weit von dem ihm zur Bearbeitung vorliegenden Text.» [GW II/III:319] (In this the dream departs more or less far from the text presented to it for working over.)

Sense 3: The Traumtext as an object of preservation (1911–1933)

In Freud's words. - «findet man immer wieder eine recht überflüssige Sorgfalt auf die getreue Erhaltung des Traumtextes verlegt, der angeblich vor den Entstellungen und Usuren der nächsten Tagesstunden bewahrt werden muß» [GW VIII:355] (one keeps finding a rather superfluous care spent on faithfully preserving the dream text, which supposedly must be guarded against the distortions and wear of the following hours of the day.) - «Hat man nämlich auf solche Weise mühselig einen Traumtext gerettet, der sonst vom Vergessen verzehrt worden wäre, so kann man sich doch leicht überzeugen, daß für den Kranken damit nichts erreicht ist.» [GW VIII:356] (If one has laboriously rescued in this way a dream text that would otherwise have been consumed by forgetting, one can easily convince oneself that nothing has been achieved for the patient.) - «Traumtexte, an denen mir gelegen ist, lasse ich von den Patienten nach der Erzählung des Traumes fixieren.» [GW VIII:379] (Dream texts that matter to me I have the patients fix after telling the dream.)

What it means. In 1911 the Traumtext exists before any writing. It is what is exposed to the distortions of the following hours and what forgetting would consume [GW VIII:355-356]. Writing on waking is a measure taken to save it, and Freud attributes that measure to some analysts: «wenn sie dem Behandelten den Auftrag geben, jeden Traum unmittelbar nach dem Erwachen schriftlich zu fixieren» [GW VIII:356] (when they instruct the person in treatment to fix every dream in writing immediately after waking). The Traumtext is therefore the wording of the remembered dream. Writing is a practice applied to it, not its referent [ii].

Its therapeutic worth depends on associations: «Zu dem Text stellen sich die Einfälle nicht ein, und der Effekt ist der nämliche, als ob der Traum nicht erhalten geblieben wäre.» [GW VIII:356] (No associations come to the text, and the effect is the same as if the dream had not been preserved.) The verdict is relative to the patient, because Freud grants the doctor's gain: «Der Arzt hat allerdings in dem einen Falle etwas erfahren, was ihm im anderen entgangen wäre.» [GW VIII:356] (The doctor has indeed learned something in the one case that he would have missed in the other.) [ii]

In 1912 Traumtexte are what the patient fixes after the telling, at Freud's request [GW VIII:379]. The medium is not stated, and one page earlier "fixieren" is used for memory [GW VIII:378]. In 1933 the Traumtext is what the dreamer preserves by writing on waking, and the same sentence calls this object «den manifesten Traum» [GW XV:14].

Across these uses the referent is the same kind of object as in Senses 1 and 4: the wording of the remembered or told dream. What sets this sense apart is its role in the argument. It is something to be kept or lost and judged by its therapeutic worth, not something being interpreted [ii].

When and where. The 1900 volume mentions records, but not the term: «es ist der einzige, über den ich keine sorgfältigen Aufzeichnungen besitze» [GW II/III:498] (it is the only one of which I have no careful records). In the texts read, the word "Traumtext" first appears in 1911 and recurs in 1912, 1916 and 1933. Not every occurrence of the word is an occurrence of this sense. The occurrences of Sense 3 are 1911 [GW VIII:355-356], 1912 [GW VIII:379] and 1933 [GW XV:14], where the text is something to be kept, fixed or lost. The 1916 occurrence [GW XI:116] plays the other role: the Traumtext is the dream that gets written down and compared with the associations, and the next sentence turns to the procedure of interpretation: «Anstatt die Deutung ganzer Träume in Angriff zu nehmen, wollen wir uns auf einzelne Traumelemente beschränken» [GW XI:116] (Instead of tackling the interpretation of whole dreams, we will restrict ourselves to single dream elements). I therefore assign it to Sense 1 [ii]. The other 1933 occurrences belong to Sense 4: the naming [GW XV:9] and the Traumtext as heard, whose sense can become clear through symbols [GW XV:13].

Freud's own or taken over. The pages do not show whether the word is Freud's coinage. Its first occurrence in the pages read comes in his report of others' concern. He attributes the care for faithful preservation to the "wissenschaftlichen" works and reports their view with the distancing word "angeblich" (supposedly), in the first quote above [GW VIII:355]. He attributes the instruction to write dreams down on waking to "manche Psychoanalytiker" (some psychoanalysts) [GW VIII:356]. On that same page he already uses the word in his own argument against the measure, in the second quote above [GW VIII:356]. He uses it again in his own voice in 1912 [GW VIII:379].

Abandoned or kept. The judgment is kept. In 1911 he writes: «Diese Maßregel ist in der Therapie überflüssig» [GW VIII:356] (This measure is superfluous in therapy). In 1933 he writes of the dreamer who fixes the dream on waking: «Wir können ihm sagen, das ist nutzlos» [GW XV:14] (We can tell him that this is useless). The 1912 passage shows that Freud did have dream texts fixed in his own practice, after the telling (see Tensions).

Not to be confused with. Sense 3 is not the act of writing, which is a measure applied to it. In 1912 the fixing follows the telling (Sense 1) [GW VIII:379]; the page does not say whether the Traumtext so fixed is the wording of the telling itself or differs from it. Nor is it the exact protocols that Freud dismisses on the same page: «Sie gehören, streng genommen, jener Scheinexaktheit an» [GW VIII:379] (Strictly speaking, they belong to that sham exactness).

Sense 4: Name for the manifest dream (1933; pair from 1913 in the pages read)

In Freud's words. - «Wir heißen, was man den Traum genannt hat, den Traumtext oder den manifesten Traum, und das, was wir suchen, sozusagen hinter dem Traum vermuten, die latenten Traumgedanken.» [GW XV:9] (What used to be called the dream we call the dream text or the manifest dream, and what we are looking for, what we suspect to lie so to speak behind the dream, the latent dream thoughts.) - «Wir beschließen, uns um das, was wir gehört haben, um den manifesten Traum, möglichst wenig zu kümmern.» [GW XV:10] (We decide to concern ourselves as little as possible with what we have heard, the manifest dream.) - «will Sie nur daran mahnen, daß Sie nicht den Wortlaut des Traumes, sondern den Sinn desselben nach seiner Deutung in Betracht ziehen» [GW XI:207] (I only want to remind you to consider not the wording of the dream but its sense after interpretation.)

What it means. In 1933 "Traumtext" is one of «zweier neuer Begriffe, Namen» (two new concepts, names) [GW XV:9]. It is joined to "der manifeste Traum" by "oder" (or), and it stands on the manifest side of a practical task: «Wir haben den manifesten in den latenten Traum umzuwandeln» [GW XV:9] (We have to transform the manifest dream into the latent one). The Traumtext is something heard [GW XV:13]. It is not described as purely verbal: «seine Bilder mögen die volle sinnliche Stärke von Wahrnehmungen zeigen» [GW XV:10] (its images may show the full sensory strength of perceptions).

When and where. In the pages read, the manifest/latent pair that the name joins appears from 1913 onward. - In 1913 interpretation leads the manifest dream back to the latent thoughts: «Diese Tagesreste deckt man auf, indem man den manifesten Traum auf die latenten Traumgedanken zurückführt» [GW X:17] (These day residues are uncovered by leading the manifest dream back to the latent dream thoughts). The items of the told dream are called «Elemente des manifesten Traumes» (elements of the manifest dream) [GW X:14] [ii]. - In 1916 the pair is named as terms: «Wir wollen das, was der Traum erzählt, den manifesten Trauminhalt nennen, das Verborgene, zu dem wir durch die Verfolgung der Einfälle kommen sollen, die latenten Traumgedanken.» [GW XI:118] (We will call what the dream tells the manifest dream content, and the hidden thing we are to reach by following the associations the latent dream thoughts.) In the same lecture series, "Wortlaut des Traumes" and «der manifeste Traum» stand in consecutive sentences, both opposed to the interpreted sense [GW XI:207]. That the two are identical is licensed by this juxtaposition, not stated [ii]. - In 1933 what is added, in the pages read, is "Traumtext" as an alternative name for the manifest dream [GW XV:9].

In the 1900 pages read, "manifester Trauminhalt" occurs only in footnotes, one of which cannot be earlier than 1917 [GW II/III:188-189]. The term "latente Traumgedanken" already occurs in the 1900 volume: «darf als ein Stück Mitteilung aus den latenten Traumgedanken aufgefaßt werden.» [GW II/III:339] (may be taken as a piece of communication from the latent dream thoughts.) The pages do not date the layer this belongs to. The two terms appear together as a pair only from 1913 in the pages read. The 1901 text has only Trauminhalt opposed to Traumgedanken [GW II/III:680].

Freud's own or taken over. In the pages read, Freud introduces the name in the first person plural as a new step: «Unser erster Schritt ist, unsere neue Einstellung zum Problem des Traums durch die Einführung zweier neuer Begriffe, Namen, festzulegen.» [GW XV:9] (Our first step is to fix our new attitude to the problem of the dream by introducing two new concepts, names.) The pages name no other source. That this use of the word is Freud's own is an inference from this first-person introduction, and the "wir" may stand for psychoanalysis rather than for Freud alone [ii].

HOLD TENSION, within the 1933 lecture and across dates. At GW XV:7 the lecture names «die grundlegende Unterscheidung von manifestem Trauminhalt und latenten Traumgedanken» (the basic distinction between manifest dream content and latent dream thoughts) as a distinction already in place. Two pages later the same lecture calls the two terms «zweier neuer Begriffe, Namen» [GW XV:9]. Grammatically, "neuer" qualifies both concepts. One of them, "die latenten Traumgedanken", is attested before 1933 in the pages read [GW II/III:339; X:17; XI:118], and the pair is named as terms in 1916 [GW XI:118]. The pages do not reconcile these statements, and both stay. It is a conjecture, not licensed by the grammar, that what Freud means as new is the "neue Einstellung" or the name "Traumtext" for the manifest side [iii].

Abandoned or kept. The 1933 lecture is the last text read. The texts read do not say more.

Not to be confused with. Sense 4 is not any of the following. - The "Fassade" (façade). In 1901 the façade does not cover the dream's content everywhere: «Der Traum erhält so eine Art Fassade, die seinen Inhalt freilich nicht an allen Stellen deckt» [GW II/III:679] (The dream thus receives a kind of façade, which admittedly does not cover its content at every point.) - The 1900 use of "manifest" for the distortion that arises in reproducing the dream: «Die Autoren haben hier das manifest arbeitende Stück der Traumentstellung geahnt oder bemerkt» [GW II/III:519] (The authors have here sensed or noticed the manifestly working piece of dream distortion). - The "latent Trauminhalt" of 1900 [GW II/III:517].

How the senses relate

Sense 1 is the base object. - Sense 2 is a different referent on one reading of GW II/III:322-323, but it is reachable only through Sense 1. The tracing of dream speeches word for word presupposes that the report keeps their wording [ii]. On the other reading, Sense 2 is a case of Sense 1 (see Sense 2). - Sense 3 has the same kind of object as Sense 1, but it is not always the told dream. In 1911 it is the dream as remembered in the hours after waking, possibly before any telling, and it is what writing would rescue from forgetting [GW VIII:355-356]. In 1912 it is fixed after the telling: the patient fixes it «nach der Erzählung des Traumes» (after the telling of the dream) [GW VIII:379]. In 1933, as in 1911, it is fixed on waking: the dreamer works against forgetting «indem er den Traum unmittelbar nach dem Erwachen schriftlich fixiert» (by fixing the dream in writing immediately after waking) [GW XV:14]. Reading Sense 3 as Sense 1 seen as something to be kept or lost is therefore an interpretation. It fits the 1912 passage and fits only loosely for 1911 and 1933 [ii]. - Sense 4 renames the object of Sense 1, «das, was wir gehört haben» (what we have heard), as the manifest dream [GW XV:10]. - In 1933 Senses 3 and 4 meet in one sentence: the preserved Traumtext and «den manifesten Traum» name the same object [GW XV:14] [ii]. On this reading, Sense 3 is, at least for 1933, not a separate referent but a separate role of the same object [ii]. The 1911 and 1912 passages do not make this identification, and they do not mention the manifest dream [GW VIII:355-356, 379].

Senses 1 and 4 can be read as one model, in which the told dream becomes, by 1933, the named manifest dream. I count them apart for two reasons, and the count is an interpretation [ii].

First, Sense 4 has a definition and a place in the manifest/latent pair that Sense 1 lacks [GW XI:118; XV:9].

Second, the 1900 account of Sense 1 explicitly separates the dream as reproduced from the dream before reproduction [GW II/III:518]. The 1933 naming, by contrast, equates what was heard with the manifest dream [GW XV:10]. The second reason is weaker than the first. The 1933 pages still treat the telling as exposed to forgetting: «Das ist als ein Versuch aufzufassen, dieses Stück zu vergessen.» [GW XV:14] So the difference lies in the naming, not in a full absence of the gap. Without the second reason, the count of Senses 1 and 4 rests on the first alone.

Sense 2 is not found after 1901 in the passages read.

Tensions held

1. How much the wording weighs. HOLD TENSION. - In 1900 every nuance counts [GW II/III:518], yet recovering the dream "does not matter" [GW II/III:522]. - In 1916 fidelity of recall is «gleichgültig» (indifferent) [GW XI:112], yet «nichts im Traum für zufällig oder gleichgültig» (nothing in the dream is accidental or indifferent) [GW XI:118]. Also in 1916, the wording is not to be taken for the sense [GW XI:207]. - In 1933 the manifest dream gets "as little as possible" attention, yet «sehr vieles an ihr für die Deutung verwertbar» (much of it usable for interpretation) is promised for later [GW XV:10].

One licensed reading is that Freud is indifferent to the apparent sense while attending to every element as a path [ii]. Freud does not state this reconciliation.

2. Dreamt versus told. HOLD TENSION, as a shift across dates and within the 1933 pages. - In 1900 Freud concedes distortion in reproduction and makes it lawful [GW II/III:518-519]. - In 1916 the distortion added by memory «auch nicht unmotiviert sein kann» (cannot be without motive either), yet fidelity is indifferent [GW XI:112]. - In 1933 the naming equates «das, was wir gehört haben» (what we have heard) with «den manifesten Traum» [GW XV:10]. Yet the same pages read an addendum as an attempt to forget: «Es ereignet sich sehr oft, daß von einem Traum zunächst ein Stück wegbleibt, das dann als Nachtrag angefügt wird. Das ist als ein Versuch aufzufassen, dieses Stück zu vergessen.» [GW XV:14] (It very often happens that a piece of a dream is at first left out and then added as an addendum. This is to be understood as an attempt to forget this piece.) They also report a forgotten dream that returns: «stellt sich der vergessene Traum plötzlich wieder ein» [GW XV:14] (the forgotten dream suddenly comes back).

These 1933 passages assume a gap between the dream and what is reported, although the naming does not mark it [ii]. Both sides stay.

3. Preserving the text. RECONNECT for 1911 against 1912; HOLD TENSION for what remains.

The passages differ in time point and purpose. - In 1911 the objection is to patients writing each dream down immediately on waking: «jeden Traum unmittelbar nach dem Erwachen schriftlich zu fixieren» [GW VIII:356] (to fix every dream in writing immediately after waking). The measure is called superfluous for therapy: «Diese Maßregel ist in der Therapie überflüssig» [GW VIII:356]. The doctor's gain is granted: «Der Arzt hat allerdings in dem einen Falle etwas erfahren, was ihm im anderen entgangen wäre.» [GW VIII:356] - In 1912 Freud has dream texts fixed after the telling [GW VIII:379], in a passage about recording material for later use as examples. The purpose of the fixing is not stated.

Once these differences are stated, 1911 and 1912 fit together. In 1933 fixing the dream on waking is again called useless, because the resistance shifts to the associations [GW XV:14].

What remains held is the contrast between this therapeutic indifference and Freud's editorial care for printed wording. He declares his one substitution in a printed wording [GW II/III:369; XI:198]. A separate item belongs here only loosely. In a footnote of 1913 Freud says he was at fault for a condensation of the material at one place, «die ich bei einer Revision der Niederschrift von der referierenden Dame korrigieren konnte» [GW X:14]. The phrase can be construed in two ways: as a revision of the lady's written record, or as a revision, made by the lady, of the written record. I read it as a revision of the written record of the case in which the reporting lady took part [ii]. On either construal it concerns a record of the case material, not a record of the dream's wording.

4. One wording or two tellings. HOLD TENSION. The repetition test reads meaning off the difference between two tellings [GW II/III:519-520], while "the" Wortlaut is to be respected [GW II/III:518]. Freud himself quotes a dream sentence in a wording that differs from the printed report [GW II/III:366-367]. In the 1913 case, the lady's interpretation, as Freud prints it, quotes a sentence of the dream in a wording that differs from the printed dream [GW X:13, 16]. That variant belongs to her report, not to Freud's own quotation.

5. Associations versus symbols (1933). RECONNECT for certain elements; HOLD TENSION for a narrow remainder. Freud states «die Unmöglichkeit, den Traum zu deuten, wenn man nicht über die dazugehörigen Assoziationen des Träumers verfügt» [GW XV:7] (the impossibility of interpreting the dream without the dreamer's associations). He qualifies this for certain elements: «so benehmen wir uns doch gegen gewisse Elemente des Trauminhalts ganz selbständig, vor allem darum, weil wir müssen, weil bei ihnen in der Regel die Assoziationen versagen.» [GW XV:12] (we nevertheless behave quite independently towards certain elements of the dream content, above all because we must, because the associations as a rule fail with them.) Once this qualification is stated, the rule and the exception fit together. What remains is a possibility that Freud marks as occasional: «kann es sich treffen, daß uns der Sinn eines Traums unmittelbar klar wird, noch vor allen Bemühungen um die Traumdeutung, sobald wir nur den Traumtext gehört haben» [GW XV:13] (it can happen that the sense of a dream becomes clear to us immediately, before any efforts at interpretation, as soon as we have merely heard the dream text). This passage speaks of the sense of a dream, not only of certain elements, but only as something that can happen. The remainder is therefore a tension between a stated impossibility and an occasional possibility, not between two general rules. I keep it held because the pages do not relate the two.

6. A "text" for a visual dream. CONJECTURE. The manifest dream consists «vorwiegend aus visuellen Bildern» (predominantly of visual images) [GW XI:120]. This may point to a gap between the dream and its verbal text, but the pages never discuss such a gap [iii].

What this entry does not claim

  • It does not claim that Freud explicitly distinguishes the dream text from the manifest content anywhere in the pages read. It also does not claim that the word "Traumtext" is equated with the manifest dream before 1933. In the pages read, the told dream is called manifest by usage in 1913 («Elemente des manifesten Traumes» [GW X:14]; the word "Traumtext" does not occur in those pages), and "Wortlaut des Traumes" stands next to "der manifeste Traum" in 1916 [GW XI:207]. "Traumtext" itself is equated with the manifest dream only in 1933 [GW XV:9].
  • It does not claim that "Traumtext" is absent elsewhere in the GW beyond the listed loci. It does not claim that the word is Freud's coinage.
  • It does not claim that the 1900 text as printed is all of 1900. Its footnotes and some passages belong to later layers that the pages do not fully date. On GW II/III:365-370, a footnote at GW II/III:365 assigns the first example to "Nachträge zur Traumdeutung" of 1911. The same page cites the later lectures: «Eine weit ausführlichere Darstellung der Traumsymbolik habe ich in meinen „Vorlesungen zur Einführung in die Psychoanalyse" (1916/17) versucht.» [GW II/III:365] The entry therefore does not date the "Traumfassade" passage at GW II/III:367 to 1900. The pages do not show which layer it belongs to.
  • It does not tie the "heiliger Text" simile to any tradition of reading. The pages name none.
  • It does not say what Freud says about the dream text in Dora, in GW VII:11, or in the "Traumfassade" passages at GW I:565 and GW XI:426.
  • Interpreting the Traumtext as a verbal translation of a visual dream is a conjecture only.
  • All dates in this entry come from the source labels unless a page prints them. This holds for 1900, 1901, 1911, 1912, 1913, 1916 and 1933. The pages of GW XI print no date. For 1933 the pages print only the volume range 1928–1933 and a pause «länger als fünfzehnjähriger» (of more than fifteen years) [GW XV:6].
