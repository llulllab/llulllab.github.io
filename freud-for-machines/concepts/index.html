Freud's basic concepts

Freud for Machines · a glossary of Llull Lab

Each entry gives a short definition first and, behind it, a long entry that counts the senses of the concept in Freud's texts, dates them, and quotes the German pages. The research tool links the entry when a question is about one of these concepts.

Concepts

  • ConsciousnessBewußtsein

    In the passages read, Bewußtsein has three senses.

  • The egodas Ich

    In the passages read, Freud uses "Ich" in six senses: the defending, repressing Ich; the Ichtriebe as a drive group; a developing Ich (Lust-Ich and Real-Ich); the Ich as seat and object of libido in narcissism; the Ich as a differentiated whole conta

  • The iddas Es

    In the passages read (1923 to 1940), "das Es" names one psychical province, which Freud introduces in Das Ich und das Es (1923) as the psychical domain into which the Ich continues and which "behaves as unconscious", naming it after Groddeck's usage.

  • The symptomdas Symptom

    In the passages read, Freud's "Symptom" carries four senses, because the texts give four different definitions: a clinical sign of a morbid process, a product of defence standing as a memory symbol of traumatic experience, a substitute for a represse

  • The dream-workdie Traumarbeit

    In the passages read, the dream-work is Freud's own name for the process that turns the latent dream thoughts into the manifest dream.

  • The manifest dream contentder manifeste Trauminhalt

    In the passages read, the manifest dream content (Freud also writes "manifester Traum" and "manifester Inhalt") is the dream set against the latent dream thoughts.

  • The dream textder Traumtext

    In the passages read, this family of terms carries four senses, and the count is itself an interpretation; all dates come from the source labels unless a page prints them.

Phenomena

Phenomena Freud studies, read from fewer texts than the concepts; each entry says which.

  • The screen memorydie Deckerinnerung

    The two texts read are Über Deckerinnerungen (1899) and chapter IV of Zur Psychopathologie des Alltagslebens (1901); in them the name is Freud's own coinage of 1899, and on our reading, which is an interpretation, it has two senses.

About the glossary

First edition, November 2026. Built from the German text of Freud's Gesammelte Werke only, read source by source; every German quotation was checked against the page it cites. References give volume and page, for example [GW X:291]. English renderings are our own. A mark [ii] means our interpretation of the passages, [iii] a synthesis across texts; unmarked sentences report what the pages say. Each long entry says which texts were read and which were not. Entries are revised as the reading continues; corrections are welcome at contact@llulllab.com.
