The manifest dream content (der manifeste Trauminhalt)

In Freud's texts. Entry · Long entry · All entries · Ask the research tool

In brief

In the passages read, Freud uses "manifester Trauminhalt" in two senses. He also writes "manifester Traum" and "manifester Inhalt" for it.

  1. The dream as given. This is the dream as it reaches the interpreter: remembered, heard, told or written down.
  2. The product of the dream-work. This is the distorted substitute for the latent dream thoughts.

The façade is closely related but is not a sense of the term. No passage read uses "manifester Trauminhalt" or "manifester Traum" to mean the apparently coherent outside of the dream. The façade has its own section below.

The manifest/latent opposition runs from Die Traumdeutung to the Abriß. - In Die Traumdeutung (first published 1900; the pages read contain later layers) it appears in a footnote whose layer cannot be dated from the pages: «die grundlegende Unterscheidung von manifestem und latentem Trauminhalt» [GW II/III:140]. - In the Abriß (1940, written 1938): «Dies ist unsere Unterscheidung eines manifesten Trauminhaltes und der latenten Traumgedanken.» [GW XVII:88].

Two points must not be missed. - The doctrine rests on the thoughts behind the dream. Freud's doctrine (in context, that wish-fulfilment is the meaning of every dream [GW II/III:139]) rests on the thought content found behind the dream by interpretation. It does not rest on assessing the manifest content: «daß unsere Lehre nicht auf der Würdigung des manifesten Trauminhalts beruht, sondern sich auf den Gedankeninhalt bezieht, welcher durch die Deutungsarbeit hinter dem Traume erkannt wird.» [GW II/III:140] (main text). This does not say that no manifest dream shows its meaning. The pages read allow three such cases: - dreams whose content is manifestly significant: «Was man sonst träumt, ist entweder manifest als psychisch bedeutsam zu erkennen, oder es ist entstellt» [GW II/III:189]; - dreams «in denen manifester und latenter Inhalt zusammenfallen, die Traumarbeit also erspart scheint» [GW II/III:656], which is hedged (see tension 6); - a 1916 façade that sometimes has meaning «indem sie einen wichtigen Bestandteil der latenten Traumgedanken wenig oder gar nicht entstellt wiederbringt» [GW XI:184]. - The façade changes its extension. In the Traumdeutung pages (layer undated), in 1916 and in 1933 it is a feature or a part of the manifest content. In 1940 the whole remembered dream is called «nur eine Fassade» [GW XVII:88].

Entry

In the passages read, the manifest dream content (Freud also writes "manifester Traum" and "manifester Inhalt") is the dream set against the latent dream thoughts. Freud calls this opposition fundamental in a Traumdeutung footnote whose layer cannot be dated, and he restates it in the Abriß (1940, written 1938). His doctrine rests on the thoughts found behind the dream by interpretation, not on assessing the manifest content, although the pages allow some cases in which a manifest dream shows its meaning, one of them hedged as only seeming to spare the dream-work. There are two senses, and both are kept to 1940. The first is the dream as given to the interpreter, that is, remembered, heard, told or written down; it is restated in Der Witz VI (1905), Über Psychoanalyse III (1910), Das Interesse an der Psychoanalyse (1913), the Vorlesungen (1916), Neue Folge XXIX (1933) and the Abriß (1940). The second is the distorted substitute produced from the latent thoughts by the dream-work; it is present from the Traumdeutung pages through Der Witz VI (1905), Über Psychoanalyse III (1910), Vorlesungen XI (1916), Neue Folge XXIX (1933) and the Abriß (1940), and in it the image of translation is qualified but not dropped. As for dates, "1900" is only the bibliographic year of the Traumdeutung, whose pages contain later layers with dated material up to 1917, while Über den Traum (bibliographic year 1901, its GW layer not datable from the pages) contains the naming of the first sense and names operations of the dream-work, and among the Traumdeutung and Über den Traum pages read it has the only explicit act of naming, which is a point about the texts and not about chronology. On our reading the two senses usually pick out one object in two roles, the given starting point of interpretation and the end product the theory explains, and their extensions can come apart in particular cases, as with a detail Freud must add to memory afterwards in 1901, or a ready-made fantasy run through only in the memory of the one who has woken (Traumdeutung, layer undated); separately, the texts do not set the dreamt and the remembered dream up as two referents, but on our reading they do not guarantee that the two coincide. Not every element is traced to the latent thoughts, and although several passages narrow the gap, a remainder is held as an unresolved tension: the tenderness traced to resistance, a remainder of critical contributions credited to the censoring instance, and the doubts the dreamer reports, which as a rule correspond to nothing latent, if they are counted as manifest elements, which is our reading. The term is Freud's, introduced by acts of naming in Über den Traum, in 1916 and in 1933, though in 1916 he says these are terms that could long have been used; he reports that earlier authors took the given dream as their only object and that popular methods replaced the remembered content by another; Sully's image of a "surface" is Sully's, not Freud's term; for the second sense the medical authors treated the dream as undivided and without sense, while popular opinion and Sully posited a sense behind it and Freud judges both close to his view (the Sully quotation and his judgement stand in a footnote of undated layer), so on our reading what is specifically his is the derivation of the content from latent thoughts by the dream-work. The façade is a related concept, not a third sense: in the Traumdeutung pages (layer undated) it is built by secondary revision or formed by a ready-made fantasy, in 1916 it is an apparently meaningful outside of the manifest dream, in 1933 it is a product of secondary revision present only "at best", and in 1940 the whole remembered dream is called only a façade; on our reading it attaches to the second sense up to 1933 and is said of the first sense in 1940, a shift from part to whole held as an unresolved tension, and whether the 1940 façade comes from secondary revision is not stated; Freud also judges that earlier authors overrated the ordering function behind it, and the pages name Goblot and, going further, Foucault. A reader must not confuse the manifest content with the latent dream thoughts, with the dream-work that produces it, with the bare noun "Trauminhalt" (which Freud also qualifies as latent), with "der Traum" used for the subject matter of the dream thoughts, with the façade before 1940, or with other uses of "Inhalt" such as the dream's "real" or "true" content; nor should the reader take the latent content for the dream's essence, an error faulted in a later footnote of uncertain date, or read the merely descriptive "conscious" of 1916, to which no theoretical construction is yet attached, as the Traumdeutung's assumption (layer not fixed) that, since the content is remembered as conscious, the privilege of the second instance is admission to consciousness; and the façade is not to be confused with the disguise (Verkleidung) of distorted wishes.

Senses. 1. The dream as given (1900 to 1940): the dream as it reaches the interpreter, remembered after waking, heard, told or written down. 2. The product of the dream-work (1900 to 1940): the distorted substitute into which the latent dream thoughts have been carried by the dream-work.

Where to read. - GW II/III:139-150 (Die Traumdeutung IV, 1900) - GW II/III:492-512 (Die Traumdeutung VI I, 1900) - GW II/III:645-670 (Über den Traum, first half, 1901) - GW XI:173-186 (Vorlesungen XI, 1916) - GW XV:6-31 (Neue Folge XXIX, 1933) - GW XVII:87-94 (Abriß ch. 5, 1940)

Long entry

The senses

I count two senses. They are defined differently: one by the way the dream is given, the other by where it comes from. They also hold different places in the theory. The first is the starting point of interpretation; the second is the object of the theory of dream-work. In most passages the two pick out the same thing [ii] (see "How the senses relate").

The façade is not counted as a sense. In the pages read it is always said of the manifest content: as a feature or part of it up to 1933, and as a description of the whole remembered dream in 1940. It is never what the term itself means.

Sense 1: The dream as given (1900 to 1940)

In Freud's words. - «Den Traum, wie er mir in der Erinnerung vorliegt, stelle ich dem durch Analyse gefundenen zugehörigen Material gegenüber» [GW II/III:654] [The dream as it lies before me in memory I set against the associated material found by analysis.] The same sentence continues: «nenne den ersteren den manifesten Trauminhalt» [GW II/III:654] [I call the first the manifest dream content]. It names the latent side «zunächst ohne weitere Scheidung» [GW II/III:654] [for now without further division]. - «Wir wollen das, was der Traum erzählt, den manifesten Trauminhalt nennen» [GW XI:118] [What the dream narrates, we will call the manifest dream content.] Here "der Traum" is the subject of «erzählt». The phrase names what the dream narrates. It does not name the dreamer's act of telling. - «Wir heißen, was man den Traum genannt hat, den Traumtext oder den manifesten Traum» [GW XV:9] [What used to be called the dream we call the dream text or the manifest dream.]

What it means. The manifest content is fixed by the way the dream is given. - In most texts that is memory after waking: «in der Erinnerung gegebenen manifesten Trauminhalt» [GW II/III:283]; «Was wir so als Traum erinnern, das heiße ich den „manifesten Trauminhalt".» [GW VI:182]; «Der Traum, wie wir ihn nach dem Erwachen erinnern, soll manifester Trauminhalt genannt werden.» [GW VIII:396]. - In 1933 it is also what the analyst hears: «Wir beschließen, uns um das, was wir gehört haben, um den manifesten Traum, möglichst wenig zu kümmern.» [GW XV:10].

Mostly the manifest content is fixed by contrast with the latent side [ii]. Some passages, however, describe it in its own right. - In 1905, before the latent thoughts are introduced: «Er ist dann ein Gefüge von meist visuellen (aber auch andersartigen) Sinneseindrücken, die uns ein Erleben vorgetäuscht haben» [GW VI:182]. - In 1916: «daß der manifeste Traum vorwiegend aus visuellen Bildern, seltener aus Gedanken und Worten besteht» [GW XI:120].

In this sense the manifest content is the indispensable starting point of interpretation. Interpretation proceeds «mit Hilfe der Assoziationen, die der Träumer selbst zu den Elementen des manifesten Inhaltes liefert» [GW XVII:92]. The content's apparent sense is set aside: «Man kümmere sich nicht um das, was der Traum zu besagen scheint» [GW XI:112].

Dreamt and remembered. The texts touch on a distinction between the dream as experienced in sleep and the dream as remembered afterwards. - In 1940 the problem is introduced through what happens during dreaming: «indem wir, solange wir träumen, den Inhalten des Traumes objektive Realität zusprechen» [GW XVII:87]. The next page defines the manifest content as what is remembered after waking [GW XVII:88]. The text does not say whether these are one manifest content or two. - The Traumdeutung (section VI A, layer undated) entertains in general terms the objection that the remembered dream is only a remnant: «Der Traum, den wir beim Erwachen erinnern, wäre dann bloß ein Rest der gesamten Traumarbeit» [GW II/III:285]. Freud grants part of the objection. He answers that the feeling of having dreamed far more than one can reproduce «sehr häufig auf einer Illusion beruht» [GW II/III:285]. The concession is partial and the answer is hedged. - A narrower passage in section VI I (layer undated) concerns one specific case: a ready-made fantasy that a waking stimulus only touches as a whole in sleep. Freud says: «Diese wird aber nicht noch im Schlaf durchlaufen, sondern erst in der Erinnerung des Erwachten.» For that case he adds: «Man hat dabei kein Mittel zur Versicherung, daß man wirklich etwas Geträumtes erinnert.» [GW II/III:502]. - The 1916 lecture treats unfaithful memory as a further distortion of the same substitute, not as a second referent [GW XI:112] (quoted under "How the senses relate").

In the passages read, then, the dreamt and the remembered dream are not set up as two referents. My reading is that the texts do not guarantee the two coincide [ii].

"Conscious" in two uses. These must be kept apart. - In the Traumdeutung (chapter IV; the main text's layer is not fixed by the pages), a descriptive fact serves as the premise of a systematic construction. The fact is that the content is remembered as conscious: «der von ihnen ausgehende manifeste Trauminhalt aber als bewußt erinnert wird». From it Freud infers: «so liegt die Annahme nicht ferne, das Vorrecht der zweiten Instanz sei eben die Zulassung zum Bewußtsein.» [GW II/III:149]. The same argument assumes «zwei psychische Mächte (Strömungen, Systeme)» [GW II/III:149]. It also likens consciousness to a sense organ: «das Bewußtsein erscheint uns als ein Sinnesorgan, welches einen anderwärts gegebenen Inhalt wahrnimmt.» [GW II/III:150]. - In 1916 the elements are called conscious in a descriptive sense only: «Natürlich dürfen wir im Gegensatz hierzu die Traumelemente selbst und die durch Assoziation neu gewonnenen Ersatzvorstellungen bewußte heißen.» [GW XI:111]. Freud adds the explicit proviso «Irgendeine theoretische Konstruktion ist mit dieser Namengebung noch nicht verbunden.» [GW XI:112].

The earlier passage builds a hypothesis about instances on the descriptive fact. The 1916 lecture withholds any such construction.

When and where. The "1900" in the heading is the bibliographic year of the Traumdeutung, not a date of the layers read.

The term is already in use in the Traumdeutung pages [GW II/III:140, 169, 283]. There it appears without an act of naming: «Stellen wir manifesten und latenten Trauminhalt einander gegenüber.» [GW II/III:140]. Those pages are layered. They contain dated material up to 1917, in footnotes and in the main text [GW II/III:172, 188, 309]. Which occurrences of the term belong to the first edition therefore cannot be decided from them.

Among the Traumdeutung and Über den Traum pages read, only Über den Traum contains an explicit act of naming: «Ich halte mich dann für berechtigt, die neue Einsicht durch Namengebung zu fixieren.» [GW II/III:654]. This is a statement about the texts, not about chronology. The year 1901 is bibliographic, and the pages print no date, so the layer of the GW text cannot be fixed from them.

The sense is restated in 1905 [GW VI:182], 1910 [GW VIII:34], 1913 [GW VIII:396], 1916 [GW XI:118], 1933 [GW XV:9] and 1940 [GW XVII:88]. Two features recur in some of these texts but not in all. Neither is shown as a change over time. - Qualified memory. It is attested in these texts: - Traumdeutung: «Ist tatsächlich ein großes Stück des Traumes für die Erinnerung verloren gegangen» [GW II/III:285]; - 1901: «in der Erinnerung verstümmelt oder auslöscht» [GW II/III:645]; and the written record of the specimen dream is qualified as possibly only what is remembered: «Dies ist der ganze Traum oder wenigstens alles, was ich von ihm erinnere.» [GW II/III:649]; - 1905: «uns meist fragmentarisch scheinenden» [GW VI:182]; - 1910: «verschwommen» [GW VIII:34]; - 1916: «wie getreu oder wie unsicher man den Traum erinnert» [GW XI:112]; - 1933, where a piece first left out and then added is read as an attempt to forget: «Es ereignet sich sehr oft, daß von einem Traum zunächst ein Stück wegbleibt, das dann als Nachtrag angefügt wird. Das ist als ein Versuch aufzufassen, dieses Stück zu vergessen.» [GW XV:14]. - Told or written down. It is attested in these texts: - Traumdeutung: «ich lasse ihn mir detailliert erzählen» [GW II/III:189]; «Der Traum füllt niedergeschrieben eine halbe Seite» [GW II/III:284]; - 1905: «den aufgeschriebenen Wortlaut eines Traumes» [GW VI:186]; - 1910: «mühselig, anscheinend willkürlich, in Worte kleiden» [GW VIII:34]; - 1916: «Schreiben wir uns den Traum nieder» [GW XI:116]; - 1933: «was wir gehört haben» [GW XV:10]. The same page treats a dream written down immediately after waking. Of the resistance in that case it says: «der Widerstand, dem er die Erhaltung des Traumtextes abgewonnen hat, verschiebt sich dann auf die Assoziation und macht den manifesten Traum für die Deutung unzugänglich.» [GW XV:14]. The sentence quoted does not itself mention writing; the link to writing down comes from its context on the page. - 1913 and 1940. In the pages read, the manifest content is given only as what is remembered after waking [GW VIII:396; GW XVII:88]. There is no qualification of memory and no mention of telling or writing down.

In 1916 the manifest dream is «das einzige uns unmittelbar Bekannte» [GW XI:184].

Freud's own or taken over. The term is Freud's: he introduces it by acts of naming [GW II/III:654; GW XI:118; GW XV:9]. In 1916 he says the terms only name something already in use: «Es scheint mir an der Zeit, zwei Termini einzuführen, die wir längst hätten verwenden können.» [GW XI:118]. He says that earlier authors took this given dream as their only object: «Diese Besonderheiten in der Auswahl des Traummaterials sind von den Autoren natürlich am manifesten Trauminhalte beobachtet worden.» [GW II/III:170]. He also reports that popular methods replaced «den erinnerten Trauminhalt durch einen anderen» [GW II/III:647]. Sully's image of a "surface" [GW II/III:141], quoted in a footnote of undated layer, is Sully's, not Freud's term.

Abandoned or kept. The sense is kept through 1940 [GW XVII:88]. In 1933 Freud states that these elementary points are unchanged: «Das alles hätte ich Ihnen schon vor fünfzehn Jahren sagen können» [GW XV:22].

Not to be confused with. - The latent dream thoughts. The 1901 text warns against a «Verwechslung des manifesten Traumes mit den latenten Traumgedanken» [GW II/III:655]. - The noun "Trauminhalt" on its own. The noun takes both qualifiers. It is "manifest" in «Stellen wir manifesten und latenten Trauminhalt einander gegenüber.» [GW II/III:140]. It is "latent" in «der durch unser Verfahren gewonnene latente Trauminhalt oder die Traumgedanken» [GW II/III:283]. The 1901 naming itself calls the latent side «den latenten Trauminhalt» [GW II/III:654]. The qualifier, not the noun, fixes the sense. Unqualified, the noun usually means the manifest content [ii]. - "Der Traum" used for what the dream is about. In one passage "der Traum" names the subject matter of the dream thoughts, not the manifest content: «der Traum handelt aber von den Gefahren sexueller Beziehungen zu niedrig stehenden Personen» [GW II/III:311].

Sense 2: The product of the dream-work (1900 to 1940)

In Freud's words. - «oder besser gesagt, der Trauminhalt erscheint uns als eine Übertragung der Traumgedanken in eine andere Ausdrucksweise» [GW II/III:283] [or better put, the dream content appears to us as a transfer of the dream thoughts into another mode of expression] - «Der manifeste Trauminhalt ist der entstellte Ersatz für die unbewußten Traumgedanken» [GW VIII:34] [The manifest dream content is the distorted substitute for the unconscious dream thoughts.] - «„Traum" kann man nichts anderes nennen als das Ergebnis der Traumarbeit, d. h. also die Form, in welche die latenten Gedanken durch die Traumarbeit überführt worden sind.» [GW XI:186] ["Dream" can be called nothing other than the result of the dream-work, that is, the form into which the latent thoughts have been carried by the dream-work.]

What it means. Here the manifest content is defined by where it comes from. The direction runs from the latent thoughts to the manifest content: «durch welche Vorgänge aus den letzteren der erstere geworden ist.» [GW II/III:283]; «Den Vorgang, der aus den letzteren den ersteren hervorgehen liess, heissen wir die Traumarbeit.» [GW XVII:88]. The 1905 text calls it a «verstümmelte und abgeänderte Umschrift» of correct psychical formations [GW VI:182].

The texts read describe it as follows: - Shorter than the latent thoughts. It is «eine Art von abgekürzter Übersetzung» [GW XI:174]. - Related many-to-many to them. «Ein manifestes Element entspricht gleichzeitig mehreren latenten, und umgekehrt kann ein latentes Element an mehreren manifesten beteiligt sein» [GW XI:176]. - Distorted by displacement. «Die Verschiebung ist das Hauptmittel der Traumentstellung» [GW XV:21].

Not every manifest element derives from the latent thoughts (see tension 4). - The tenderness in the uncle dream is traced to the dreamer's resistance [GW II/III:147]. - The revising function «Beiträge zum Trauminhalt liefern kann» [GW II/III:493]. - The doubts the dreamer reports correspond as a rule to nothing latent: «Diesen Zweifeln und Unsicherheiten entspricht in der Regel in den latenten Traumgedanken nichts» [GW XI:181]. Counting these reported doubts among the manifest elements is my reading [ii].

When and where. The "1900" in the heading is the bibliographic year of the Traumdeutung, not a date of the layers read.

The sense is present from the Traumdeutung pages [GW II/III:283] to 1940 [GW XVII:90]. In the pages read, neither the list of operations nor the agent of distortion shows a simple succession over time.

Operations. - Traumdeutung, section VI I (layer undated). Four conditions of dream formation are named together. Besides condensation, they are «der Zwang, der Zensur auszuweichen, und die Rücksicht auf Darstellbarkeit» [GW II/III:503], and secondary revision is called the «vierten Momente» [GW II/III:495]. - Über den Traum. Only the first half was read, and it breaks off at GW II/III:670, so no complete list can be read off it. Those pages name dramatisation and condensation together: «Neben der Verwandlung eines Gedankens in eine Situation (der „Dramatisierung") ist die Verdichtung der wichtigste und eigentümlichste Charakter der Traumarbeit.» [GW II/III:666]. Displacement follows on the next page: «Kein anderer Vorgang trägt soviel dazu bei, um den Sinn des Traumes zu verbergen» [GW II/III:667]. - 1916. Four operations: «verdichten, verschieben, plastisch darstellen und das Ganze dann einer sekundären Bearbeitung unterziehen» [GW XI:185].

The pages read do not show a change in the list.

Agent of distortion. The namings overlap. - Traumdeutung (layer undated). Censorship: one power «eine Zensur an diesem Traumwunsch übt und durch diese Zensur eine Entstellung seiner Äußerung erzwingt» [GW II/III:149]. Censorship and resistance are joined: «daß sie der Zensur des Widerstandes entzogen seien.» [GW II/III:314]. Displacement comes about through «jener Zensur, der endopsychischen Abwehr» [GW II/III:314]. - 1910. «diese Entstellung ist das Werk von abwehrenden Kräften des Ichs, Widerständen» [GW VIII:34]. The word "Zensur" does not occur in these pages. - 1913. «Wir erfahren, daß es eine Zensur, eine prüfende Instanz in uns gibt» [GW VIII:397]. The text does not say in so many words that this censorship causes the distortion of the manifest content. That causal link would be [iii]. - 1916, Lecture VII. Resistance, linked to distortion as a matter of more and less: «ein großer Widerstand bringt aber große Entstellungen des Unbewußten» [GW XI:115]. The text gives a correlation of greater and smaller, not a stated proportion. "Zensur" does not occur in that lecture. - 1916, Lecture XI. Displacement: «wir wissen ja, sie ist ganz das Werk der Traumzensur.» [GW XI:177]. - 1933. Resistance and censorship are identified: «daß der Widerstand gegen die Traumdeutung, das Anzeichen der Traumzensur, nichts anderes ist als der Verdrängungswiderstand» [GW XV:15]. The censorship is ascribed to the remainder of maintained repression: «Der ganze vom schlafenden Ich zugelassene Prozeß der Traumbildung steht aber unter der Bedingung der Zensur, die von dem Rest der aufrechterhaltenen Verdrängung ausgeübt wird.» [GW XV:19]. Later in the same lecture the censorship is assigned to the super-ego: «Indem wir nun auch die Traumzensur als eine Leistung dieser Instanz erkannten» [GW XV:29]. - 1940. «durch das Sträuben des Ichs jene Veränderungen erfährt, die wir als die Traumentstellung kennen» [GW XVII:88]. "Zensur" does not occur in these pages.

What stays across these texts is the derived, substitutive status of the manifest content. In 1933 and 1940 the dream is also characterised as a compromise: «Der Traum, der so entsteht, ist bereits eine Kompromißbildung» [GW XV:19]; «dass das Ergebnis der Traumarbeit ein Kompromiss ist» [GW XVII:90].

Freud's own or taken over. In the pages read, Freud presents the derivation of the content from latent thoughts by the dream-work as his own. He sets it against an earlier view that sought the explanation «in ihm selbst» [GW VI:182]. The views of others reported in the pages read fall into two groups. - The medical authors treat the dream as undivided and without sense: «Das Geträumte hat nicht mehr Anspruch auf Sinn und Bedeutung» [GW II/III:646]. The same holds for medical research in 1913 [GW VIII:395] and the medical view of absurdity in 1916 [GW XI:181]. - Popular opinion and Sully do posit something behind the dream's content, and Freud presents both as close to his view. - Popular opinion holds that a sense can be gained by interpretation «und der durch irgend ein Verfahren der Deutung aus seinem oft verworrenen und rätselhaften Inhalt gewonnen werden könne» [GW II/III:647]. Freud judges «daß nicht die ärztliche, sondern die laienhafte, halb noch im Aberglauben befangene Auffassung des Traumes der Wahrheit nahe kommt» [GW II/III:647]. - Sully writes: «like some palimpsest, the dream discloses beneath its worthless surface-characters traces of an old and precious communication» [GW II/III:141]. Freud says: «Keine der in der Literatur niedergelegten Äußerungen kommt aber dieser meiner Aufstellung so sehr entgegen» [GW II/III:140]. Both the Sully quotation and Freud's judgement stand in a footnote of undated layer.

A sense behind the dream's content is therefore not Freud's alone in the pages read. What the pages show as specifically his is the derivation of the content by the dream-work from latent thoughts [ii].

Abandoned or kept. The sense is kept. The image of translation is qualified, not dropped: «nicht eine Übersetzung Wort für Wort oder Zeichen für Zeichen» [GW XI:177].

Not to be confused with. - The dream-work itself. The dream-work is the process; the manifest content is its product: «den Prozeß, der die Entstellung der unbewußten Traumgedanken zum manifesten Trauminhalt herbeigeführt hat. Wir heißen diesen Prozeß die „Traumarbeit".» [GW VIII:35]. - The latent content taken as the dream's essence. A footnote faults analysts who «suchen das Wesen des Traums in diesem latenten Inhalt und übersehen dabei den Unterschied zwischen latenten Traumgedanken und Traumarbeit.» [GW II/III:510]. The footnote's own wording is retrospective: «Nun da sich wenigstens die Analytiker damit befreundet haben, für den manifesten Traum seinen durch Deutung gefundenen Sinn einzusetzen» [GW II/III:510]. It is therefore a later addition of uncertain date.

The façade: a related concept, not a sense of the term

In Freud's words. - «an den Traum gleichsam eine Fassade anzubauen» [GW II/III:495] [to build, as it were, a façade onto the dream] - «Aber diese gleichsam rationalisierende Tätigkeit, die im besten Falle den Traum mit einer glatten Fassade versieht, wie sie zu seinem wirklichen Inhalt nicht passen kann» [GW XV:21] [But this as it were rationalising activity, which at best provides the dream with a smooth façade that cannot fit its real content] - «dass das, was wir als Traum nach dem Erwachen erinnern, nicht der wirkliche Traumvorgang ist, sondern nur eine Fassade, hinter welcher sich dieser verbirgt» [GW XVII:88] [that what we remember as the dream after waking is not the real dream process but only a façade behind which that process hides]

Why it is not a sense of the term. No passage read uses "manifester Trauminhalt" or "manifester Traum" to mean the apparently coherent outside of the dream. - Up to 1933 the façade is something the manifest dream has or contains: «wo sie fast unverändert den Trauminhalt oder wenigstens die Traumfassade bilden dürfen» [GW II/III:497]; «Selbst wenn er eine anscheinend sinnvolle Außenseite hat» [GW XI:184], where "er" is the manifest dream; «die im besten Falle den Traum mit einer glatten Fassade versieht» [GW XV:21]. - In 1940 "Fassade" is said of the whole remembered dream, which is Sense 1 [GW XVII:88].

What it is, by date. - Traumdeutung, section VI I (layer undated). Building the façade is the work of the fourth factor, secondary revision [GW II/III:495]. That work is largely spared «daß im Material der Traumgedanken ein solches Gebilde, seiner Verwendung harrend, bereits fertig vorgefunden wird» [GW II/III:495], namely a ready-made fantasy. Such a fantasy may form «den Trauminhalt oder wenigstens die Traumfassade» [GW II/III:497]. Thoroughly revised dreams are «Träume, die sozusagen schon einmal gedeutet worden sind» [GW II/III:494]. The interpretive rule follows: «den scheinbaren Zusammenhang im Traum, als seiner Herkunft nach verdächtig, in allen Fällen unbeachtet zu lassen» [GW II/III:504]. - 1916. The apparently meaningful outside can have arisen through distortion: «daß diese durch Traumentstellung entstanden sein [...] kann» [GW XI:184]. Sometimes it is meaningful because it brings back latent thoughts with little or no distortion: «indem sie einen wichtigen Bestandteil der latenten Traumgedanken wenig oder gar nicht entstellt wiederbringt» [GW XI:184]. Secondary revision is introduced only on the next page [GW XI:185]. That it produces the 1916 façade is a reading the text does not state. - 1933. The façade is a product of secondary revision and is present only «im besten Falle» [GW XV:21]. Without that revision the dream «alle seine Risse und Sprünge offen zur Schau trägt» [GW XV:22]. - 1940. The façade is the whole remembered dream [GW XVII:88]. The Abriß pages never link it to the «sekundäre Bearbeitung» they name two pages later [GW XVII:90].

When and where. In the pages read the façade appears in the Traumdeutung pages [GW II/III:495, 497], in 1916 [GW XI:184], in 1933 [GW XV:21] and in 1940 [GW XVII:88]. It is absent from the 1901, 1905, 1910 and 1913 pages and from 1916 Lecture VII. The compound "Traumfassade" occurs only once [GW II/III:497].

Freud's own or taken over. In the pages read the image is used by Freud, marked by «gleichsam» [GW II/III:495], and is attributed to no one else. The pages do not show that Freud coined it [iii]. The ordering function behind it had been recognised by earlier authors. In Freud's judgement, because it was the only factor of dream formation they recognised, it came to be overrated: «Es konnte dann nicht ausbleiben, daß dieses einzig erkannte Moment der Traumbildung in seiner Bedeutung überschätzt wurde, so daß man ihm die ganze Leistung, den Traum geschaffen zu haben, zuschob.» [GW II/III:506]. The pages name particular authors for this view, Goblot and, going further, Foucault: «Diese Schöpfung sollte sich im Moment des Erwachens vollziehen, wie es Goblot und noch weitergehend Foucault annehmen» [GW II/III:506].

Abandoned or kept. The façade is kept, but its extension changes. Up to 1933 it is a feature or part of the content; in 1940 it is the whole remembered dream [ii].

Not to be confused with. - Sense 1 as a whole, before 1940. - The disguise of distorted wishes. Page 147 uses the participle: «Wo die Wunscherfüllung unkenntlich, verkleidet ist» [GW II/III:147]. Page 148 uses the noun: «Je strenger die Zensur waltet, desto weitgehender wird die Verkleidung» [GW II/III:148].

How the senses relate

In most passages Senses 1 and 2 pick out the same thing [ii]. - In the Abriß one sentence calls the remembered dream the manifest content, and the next calls the dream-work what produced it [GW XVII:88]. - The 1901 naming passage likewise sets the remembered dream against the material found by analysis [GW II/III:654]. - The 1916 lecture reads the two as one model, in which memory's infidelity is simply a further distortion of the same substitute: «War also unsere Erinnerung ungetreu, so hat sie einfach an diesem Ersatz eine weitere Entstellung vorgenommen» [GW XI:112] [ii].

I count them separately for two reasons. Both are interpretive [ii]. - Two roles. The 1933 lecture sets two tasks on one and the same manifest dream: «Wir haben den manifesten in den latenten Traum umzuwandeln und anzugeben, wie im Seelenleben des Träumers der letztere zum ersteren geworden ist.» [GW XV:9]. In the first task the manifest dream is the given starting point; in the second it is the end product to be explained. Freud assigns the two tasks to different kinds of work: «Das erste Stück ist eine praktische Aufgabe, es fällt der Traumdeutung zu, braucht eine Technik; das zweite eine theoretische» [GW XV:9]. The text names one object in two roles. The step from two roles to two senses is my inference. - Extensions that can come apart in particular cases. In 1901 a detail must be added to the memory afterwards: «das ich der Erinnerung nachträglich einfügen muß» [GW II/III:662]. In the Traumdeutung (layer undated) the clearest case is a narrow one. It concerns a ready-made fantasy that is only touched in sleep and run through «erst in der Erinnerung des Erwachten», for which one has «kein Mittel zur Versicherung, daß man wirklich etwas Geträumtes erinnert» [GW II/III:502]. The general statement on the remembered dream as a remnant is hedged (see Sense 1) [GW II/III:285].

The façade is not a third sense. Up to 1933 it is related to Sense 2: it is a feature of the content arising within dream formation, depending on the date by secondary revision, by a ready-made fantasy taken up whole, by distortion, or, in some cases in 1916, by carrying latent thoughts over with little or no distortion (see the façade section) [ii]. In 1940 it is said of Sense 1 [ii]. Whether the 1940 façade is meant as the product of secondary revision is not stated; a reader could only conjecture that it is [iii].

Tensions held

Passages from the Traumdeutung are cited below without a firm date. The pages contain later insertions in footnotes and in the main text, so their layer cannot be fixed from the pages.

  1. Façade as part or as whole. HOLD TENSION. In the Traumdeutung (section VI I, layer undated) a fantasy can form the content «oder wenigstens die Traumfassade» [GW II/III:497]. In 1933 the façade appears only «im besten Falle» [GW XV:21]. In 1940 the whole remembered dream is «nur eine Fassade» [GW XVII:88].

  2. When secondary revision acts. HOLD TENSION, between an undated layer and 1933. - Traumdeutung, section VI I (layer undated). Freud rejects a provisional content reworked afterwards: «Man muß eher annehmen, daß die Anforderungen dieser Instanz von allem Anfang an eine der Bedingungen abgeben, denen der Traum genügen soll» [GW II/III:503]. - 1933. Revision is added «nachdem der Traum als Wahrnehmungsobjekt vor dem Bewußtsein aufgetaucht ist» [GW XV:21].

Because the earlier passage cannot be dated from the pages, the tension is not established as a change over time.

  1. Disregard or use of the manifest dream. RECONNECT. - Within 1933. Attending «möglichst wenig» [GW XV:10] and using «auch rein formale Züge des manifesten Traums» [GW XV:27] hold together as successive phases. - Within 1916. One passage says «Die Form der Träume ist also an sich keineswegs bedeutungslos und fordert selbst zur Deutung heraus.» [GW XI:180]. Another says «Es muß uns gleichgültig erscheinen, ob er gut komponiert oder in eine Reihe von Einzelbildern ohne Zusammenhang aufgelöst ist.» [GW XI:184]. The same page qualifies the second passage: «Aber wir können das nicht wissen, ehe wir den Traum der Deutung unterzogen und dadurch ein Urteil gewonnen haben, welches Maß von Entstellung Platz gegriffen hat.» [GW XI:184]. Restated [ii]: the composition is indifferent as a guide taken in advance, and whether a feature of the dream's form or outside means anything is settled only by interpretation. - Across texts. The Traumdeutung rule (section VI I, layer undated) is to leave the apparent connection unattended «in allen Fällen» [GW II/III:504]. In 1916: «Andere Male hat auch diese Fassade des Traumes ihre Bedeutung» [GW XI:184]. Restated [ii]: both texts tell the interpreter not to trust the apparent connection in advance. The 1916 text adds that interpretation may afterwards find the façade meaningful.

  2. Origin of the elements. HOLD TENSION. - One Traumdeutung passage (layer undated) says the elements are formed «aus der ganzen Masse der Traumgedanken» [GW II/III:290]. - Other passages trace some elements elsewhere: to the revising function [GW II/III:493], to resistance [GW II/III:147] (both Traumdeutung, layer undated), and to the censorship, in the case of the doubts the dreamer reports [GW XI:181]. Counting those reported doubts among the manifest elements is my reading [ii]. - Several passages narrow the gap.

    • Even the inserted links mostly go back to latent material: «zumeist lassen sich die Schaltgedanken immerhin auf Material in den Traumgedanken zurückführen, welches aber weder durch seine eigene Wertigkeit noch durch Überdeterminierung Anspruch auf Aufnahme in den Traum erheben könnte.» [GW II/III:494].
    • Most criticism within the dream is latent material: «Die meisten dieser Regungen von Kritik im Traum sind nicht gegen den Trauminhalt gerichtet, sondern erweisen sich als übernommene und passend verwendete Teile des Traummaterials» [GW II/III:492]. GW II/III:493 concerns only the remainder.
    • In 1916, judgements in the dream are mostly latent material: «Was sich im Traum von Urteilsäußerungen, von Kritik, Verwunderung, Folgerung findet, das sind nicht Leistungen der Traumarbeit, nur sehr selten Äußerungen des Nachdenkens über den Traum, sondern zumeist Stücke der latenten Traumgedanken» [GW XI:185].
    • In 1901 (layer of the GW text not datable from the pages), the apparent difference of subject matter between content and thoughts is called a mere appearance, with a hedge: «Dies ist freilich nur ein Schein, der sich bei genauerer Untersuchung verflüchtigt [...] Aber es bleibt von der Verschiedenheit doch etwas bestehen.» [GW II/III:667].

What remains unreconciled is threefold: the tenderness traced to resistance [GW II/III:147], the remainder of critical contributions credited to the censoring instance [GW II/III:493], and the reported doubts, which correspond as a rule to nothing latent [GW XI:181], if they are counted as manifest elements [ii].

  1. Agent of distortion. RECONNECT, with a remainder. The texts read relate several of the namings themselves. - The Traumdeutung joins censorship and resistance: «der Zensur des Widerstandes» [GW II/III:314]. - The 1933 lecture identifies the resistance to interpretation as the sign of the censorship and as repression-resistance [GW XV:15]. It then makes the censorship a function of the super-ego [GW XV:29].

The remainder: the pages read do not relate the ego's defensive forces of 1910 [GW VIII:34] and the ego's Sträuben of 1940 [GW XVII:88] to the super-ego of 1933. The 1913 censorship [GW VIII:397] is not explicitly tied to distortion.

  1. Undistorted dreams. RECONNECT. The first passage is itself hedged with «scheint»: «in denen manifester und latenter Inhalt zusammenfallen, die Traumarbeit also erspart scheint» [GW II/III:656]. The later passage says «Auch in dieser Gruppe wird also eine Art Umwandlung — die man als Traumarbeit bezeichnen darf — nicht völlig vermißt» [GW II/III:660]. Restated [ii]: in this group the dream-work only seems to be spared, and a minimal transformation remains.

  2. The translation image. RECONNECT. - It is corrected within one page: «oder besser gesagt, der Trauminhalt erscheint uns als eine Übertragung der Traumgedanken in eine andere Ausdrucksweise» [GW II/III:283]. - It is qualified again in 1916: «Die Traumarbeit besorgt also eine sehr ungewöhnliche Art von Transkription der Traumgedanken, nicht eine Übersetzung Wort für Wort oder Zeichen für Zeichen» [GW XI:177].

What this entry does not claim

  • It does not claim that any page read assigns the manifest content itself to a psychic system. Several passages come near. Each places the process of dream formation, its material, or one stage of the dream, and none names a system to which the content belongs.
  • Traumdeutung IV (layer not fixed by the pages). From the content being remembered as conscious, Freud infers that admission to consciousness is the second instance's privilege [GW II/III:149]. This links the manifest content to that admission.
    1. The unconscious wish acts on the material of the dream thoughts, and «Letzteres wird dabei gleichsam ins Unbewußte herabgezogen» [GW VI:184]. More exactly, it is given treatment «wie sie auf der Stufe der unbewußten Denkvorgänge vorkömmlich und für diese Stufe charakteristisch ist» [GW VI:184]. This places the material and its treatment, not the manifest content.
    1. The processes of the dream-work are placed in one sentence that contains two formulations: «welche ungeahnten psychischen Vorgänge im Unbewußten, oder genau ausgedrückt, zwischen zwei gesonderten psychischen Systemen wie dem Bewußten und dem Unbewußten, möglich sind» [GW VIII:35]. This places the process, not its product.
    1. The study of the dream-work «zeigt, daß in dem System der unbewußten Seelentätigkeit Prozesse von ganz anderer Art ablaufen als im Bewußtsein wahrgenommen werden» [GW VIII:397]. This places processes inferred from the dream-work, not the manifest content.
    1. Dream formation is framed as the conflict of two instances: «Daß der Konflikt zweier psychischer Instanzen, die wir — ungenau — als das unbewußte Verdrängte und das Bewußte bezeichnen, überhaupt unser Seelenleben beherrscht» [GW XV:15]. The dream-work «hat uns den ersten Einblick in die Vorgänge gegeben, die sich im unbewußten System abspielen» [GW XV:17]. Both place the process. Secondary revision is added «nachdem der Traum als Wahrnehmungsobjekt vor dem Bewußtsein aufgetaucht ist» [GW XV:21]. This places the dream before consciousness at one stage, without assigning it to the conscious or the preconscious system.
    1. The material «wie unbewusstes Material aus dem Es, ursprüngliches und verdrängtes, sich dem Ich aufdrängt, vorbewusst wird und durch das Sträuben des Ichs jene Veränderungen erfährt, die wir als die Traumentstellung kennen» [GW XVII:88]. Preconscious thoughts «werden im Laufe der Traumarbeit so behandelt, als ob sie unbewusste Anteile des Es wären» [GW XVII:89]. «Die Traumarbeit ist also im wesentlichen ein Fall von unbewusster Bearbeitung vorbewusster Gedankenvorgänge.» [GW XVII:90]. These place the material and the work, not the manifest content.
  • HOLD TENSION, as a difference of formulation and not as a clean opposition between dates. Two ways of placing the processes occur side by side: "in the unconscious" and "between two systems" or "between two instances". Both are present within the 1910 sentence, which corrects «im Unbewußten» to «zwischen» [GW VIII:35]. Both are also present within the 1933 lecture, which speaks of the conflict of two instances [GW XV:15] and of processes «im unbewußten System» [GW XV:17]. 1913 has the form "in the system of unconscious activity" [GW VIII:397]. The pages read do not relate these formulations.
  • It does not claim that the manifest content is worthless. The manifest content, and in most texts read its elements, remain the starting point of interpretation. In 1913 the pages speak only of interpretive work on the manifest content, not on its elements [GW VIII:396].
  • It does not count the façade as a sense of the term, and it does not read the 1940 façade back into the earlier texts.
  • It does not treat "Traumfassade" as Freud's standing term, since the compound occurs once in the pages read [GW II/III:497].
  • It does not merge other uses of "Inhalt" into this concept. Two examples are «wirklichen Inhalt» [GW XV:22] and the Lynkeus passage's «dem wahren Inhalt des Traumes» [GW II/III:314].
  • It makes no claim about Freud's work beyond the pages listed below, and it uses no Standard Edition renderings.
