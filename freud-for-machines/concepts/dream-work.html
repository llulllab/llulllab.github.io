The dream-work (die Traumarbeit)

In Freud's texts. Entry · Long entry · All entries · Ask the research tool

In brief

In the passages read, “Traumarbeit” carries two senses. The count is my reading [ii]; see “The senses”.

  1. The transformation process. This is the process that turns the latent dream thoughts into the manifest dream. It is attested from 1900, for example «durch welche Vorgänge aus den letzteren der erstere geworden ist» [GW II/III:283], to 1940 [GW XVII:88]. The texts characterise this one process in three ways: - (a) by its operations; - (b) as a case of the unconscious primary process. This runs from the seventh chapter of the dream book (1900) to the Abriß (1940), which says «im wesentlichen ein Fall von unbewusster Bearbeitung vorbewusster Gedankenvorgänge» [GW XVII:90]; - (c) as a sleep-preserving, wish-fulfilling performance. This becomes the dream-work’s “only” function in 1913 [GW VIII:398] and its “essential achievement” in 1940 [GW XVII:92].
  2. A one-off 1900 use. Inside an objection, the word appears to name the whole of the night’s dreaming [GW II/III:285] [ii].

The positions on “translation only” versus “something added” are dated, but they do not form a single line of development: - In 1900 the dream-work does “nothing but a translation” [GW II/III:447]. Yet the same text has it creating contacts «wo sie nicht vorhanden sind» [GW II/III:345]. - In 1916 it “never limits itself to translating” and “regularly adds” the wish [GW XI:229–230]. The same lecture series also limits it: its activity is exhausted by four operations [GW XI:185]. - In 1901 and 1923 it is denied a creative character [GW II/III:680], [GW XIII:217].

These positions are held as a tension under “Tensions held”.

A reader must not miss two separations.

  • From material and from interpretation. The dream-work is kept apart from its material, the dream thoughts, and from the interpreter’s work. Freud says that interpretation «will die Traumarbeit aufheben» [GW XI:174].
  • Sekundäre Bearbeitung versus Sekundärvorgang. Sekundäre Bearbeitung (secondary revision) is not Sekundärvorgang (secondary process) [ii]. The first is called «jenes Moment der Traumarbeit» [GW II/III:505]. The second names the process that results under the inhibition of the second system [GW II/III:607]. The pages do not themselves set the two side by side; the separation is licensed by these two passages.

Entry

In the passages read, the dream-work is Freud's own name for the process that turns the latent dream thoughts into the manifest dream. He introduces it in Die Traumdeutung (1900) through a task he presents as new, and he first defines it formally, in the passages read, in Über den Traum (1901). The wish-fulfilling, sleep-preserving aspect is also his own in the pages read, while the 1913 dictum that the dream is the guardian of sleep is printed in quotation marks without attribution and has the dream, not the dream-work, as its subject. What he credits to others is partial: he says that most authors noticed secondary revision and that for them it was the only factor of dream formation they recognised; he treats earlier formulas as happy anticipations of his primary-process account; and in 1905 he presents the concept of the unconscious behind that account as shared with Lipps. His remark on Scherner, by contrast, applies Freud's own term to what Scherner attributed to the dream without showing that Scherner used the word.

On our reading the word has two senses with different referents. The first is the transformation process. The second is a single 1900 use (GW II/III:285) that appears to name the whole of the night's dreaming as a quantity that could in principle be remembered, though it may instead mean the process in its full extent. This second use stands on the same page as a use of the process sense and must not be confused with it, and it is not found again in the passages read. It occurs inside an objection that Freud partly grants before answering, and the pages do not settle whose voice it represents.

The process sense is kept in every text read: - Die Traumdeutung (1900, GW II/III); - Über den Traum (1901, GW II/III:645-700); - Der Witz (1905, GW VI:181-205); - Das Interesse an der Psychoanalyse (1913, GW VIII:394-398); - the Vorlesungen (1916, GW XI); - the Metapsychologische Ergänzung zur Traumlehre (1917, GW X:413-426); - "Psychoanalyse" und "Libidotheorie" (1923, GW XIII:216-218); - the Neue Folge (1933, GW XV), where the core is said to be unchanged; - the Abriß (1940, written 1938, GW XVII:87-94).

Within that sense the texts characterise one process in three ways, which are aspects and not separate senses, and each is attested from 1900 to 1940. The first aspect is the operations. The second is unconscious primary-process working, which grows out of the operations when chapter VII of the 1900 book recasts those of chapter VI, and which coexists with them rather than replacing them. The third is a wish-fulfilling, sleep-preserving performance, which coexists with both. On our reading it gains weight until it is the dream-work's "only" function in 1913 and its "essential achievement" in 1940. The 1933 formula "the dream is the attempt at a wish-fulfilment," repeated in 1940, has the dream and not the dream-work as its subject. The texts link the operations to symptom formation from 1900 onwards, but they never say how the three aspects fit together, so any unified model of them would be a conjecture.

Condensation and displacement belong to the dream-work in every text read that covers the operations, but much else is held as tension: - Which aspect is essential. 1916 says the dream-work consists essentially in turning thoughts into hallucinatory experience, while 1940 calls both the primary-process description and the wish-fulfilment essential within two pages. - The ranking and count of the operations. This includes where the step from optative to present belongs. - Whether secondary revision belongs to the dream-work. It is included in 1900 and 1916 and excluded in 1923. The 1900 text also pulls the other way, and 1917 says that secondary revision is what every perceptual content undergoes. On our reading, 1933 leaves the question open, and 1940 places secondary revision within the compromise that the dream-work produces. - Whether the dream-work only translates or adds something. 1900 calls it nothing but a translation, 1901 and 1923 deny it a creative character, and 1916 has it regularly add the unconscious wish. The 1900 and 1916 texts each also pull the other way. - Other tensions. These include the scope of the term in 1901, who performs the work, whether regression lies inside or after it, mechanism versus intention, and identity with symptom formation.

A reader must keep the dream-work apart from the following: - its material, the dream thoughts; - the phantasy activity that forms those thoughts, which 1900 calls something entirely different; - the work of interpretation, which aims to cancel it; - distortion, which is its result; - the unconscious wish, which in 1916 the dream-work is said to add and which supplies the energy for dream formation, although the texts do not say how the adding relates to the supplying; 1933 calls the wish the true creator of the dream, in a sentence that does not mention the dream-work; - symbolism, which in 1900 and 1901 the dream-work uses but does not produce; 1923 and 1933 place symbols differently without saying whether the dream-work produces them, a held tension.

On our reading, secondary revision (sekundäre Bearbeitung), which 1900 calls a factor of the dream-work, must not be confused with the secondary process (Sekundärvorgang). The two are not wholly unrelated, though: 1900 identifies the function behind secondary revision, with a hedge, as normal thinking, and normal thinking is the thinking whose mode the same text calls the secondary process.

Senses. 1. The transformation process, 1900 to 1940: the process that turns the latent dream thoughts into the manifest dream, characterised by its operations, as unconscious primary-process working, and as a wish-fulfilling, sleep-preserving performance. 2. The whole of the night's dreaming, 1900 only (GW II/III:285): on our reading, the word names in one objection the dreaming as a quantity that might equal the dream thoughts in extent if fully remembered, though it may instead mean the process in its full extent; it is not found again in the passages read.

Where to read. - GW II/III:283-354, 444-470, 492-512, 593-613 (Die Traumdeutung, 1900; the printed text is layered and cites works of 1905, 1909, 1910 and 1914) - GW II/III:645-700 (Über den Traum, 1901) - GW VIII:394-398 (Das Interesse an der Psychoanalyse, 1913) - GW XI:173-186, 218-232 (Vorlesungen, Lectures XI and XIV, 1916) - GW XIII:216-218 ("Psychoanalyse" und "Libidotheorie", 1923) - GW XVII:87-94 (Abriß, chapter 5, 1940, written 1938)

The entry is drawn from the long entry below, which gives each sense with Freud's words, dates and pages.

Long entry

The senses

I count two senses [ii].

Sense 1 is the process that turns the latent dream thoughts into the manifest dream. The texts characterise this one process in three ways: - by its operations (aspect a); - as a case of unconscious, primary-process working (aspect b); - by its function of fulfilling a wish and preserving sleep (aspect c).

These are aspects of one sense, not three senses. Within single texts, the same word is used for the same process under all three characterisations: - In 1916 the dream-work is «jenen psychischen Vorgang, der aus den latenten Traumgedanken den manifesten Traum formt» [GW XI:229]. In the same passage, «Sondern sie nimmt regelmäßig etwas hinzu» [GW XI:230]. - In one 1940 chapter, the process definition [GW XVII:88], the metapsychological characterisation [GW XVII:90] and the functional one [GW XVII:92] stand within four pages.

No page read uses “Traumarbeit” for the function or for the primary process as opposed to the transformation. At different dates the texts mark different aspects as what the dream-work “essentially” is or does. That mismatch is a tension within the one sense, and it is held under “Tensions held”.

Sense 2 has a different referent. In one 1900 objection the word appears to name the whole of the night’s dreaming [GW II/III:285].

The texts never state how the three aspects of sense 1 fit together, so any unified model of them remains [iii].

Sense 1, aspect (a): Transformation of latent thoughts into the manifest dream, by its operations (1900 to 1940)

In Freud’s words. - «Den Vorgang der Verwandlung vom latenten zum manifesten Trauminhalt werde ich die Traumarbeit nennen.» [GW II/III:654] (I shall call the process of transformation from the latent to the manifest dream content the dream-work.) - «Als Traumarbeit wird die ganze Summe der umwandelnden Vorgänge zu bezeichnen sein, welche die latenten Traumgedanken in den manifesten Traum überführt haben.» [GW VI:183] (The whole sum of the transforming processes that have carried the latent dream thoughts over into the manifest dream is to be called dream-work.) - «mehr als verdichten, verschieben, plastisch darstellen und das Ganze dann einer sekundären Bearbeitung unterziehen, kann sie nicht» [GW XI:185] (it can do no more than condense, displace, represent plastically, and then submit the whole to a secondary revision.)

What it means. The dream-work is a directed process. It runs from the dream thoughts, which are «ohne weiteres verständlich» [GW II/III:283], to the manifest content, which is «eine Übertragung der Traumgedanken in eine andere Ausdrucksweise» [GW II/III:283]. The dream-work is known only by comparing the two. The 1905 text says the concept «ergibt sich» from that comparison [GW VI:183].

The dream-work is defined by its operations, not by thinking. Freud says «Sie denkt, rechnet, urteilt überhaupt nicht, sondern sie beschränkt sich darauf umzuformen» [GW II/III:511]. Apparent judgments in a dream are «nicht etwa als Denkleistung der Traumarbeit aufzufassen» [GW II/III:447].

In 1916 Freud states what the work consists in: «die Traumarbeit bestehe wesentlich in der Umsetzung von Gedanken in ein halluzinatorisches Erleben» [GW XI:218]. This sentence describes the form the transformation takes, not its purpose.

When and where. In 1900 the term is introduced through a task, not through a definitional sentence [GW II/III:283]. The operations are built up section by section: - condensation [GW II/III:284–285], first stated as «Die Annahme einer Verdichtung in der Traumarbeit» [GW II/III:285]; - displacement, «geradezu das wesentliche Stück der Traumarbeit» [GW II/III:313]; - consideration of representability, «ein drittes Moment» [GW II/III:349]; - secondary revision as the fourth factor, «jenes Moment der Traumarbeit» [GW II/III:505]; - the suppression of affects [GW II/III:470].

The same 1900 text divides the mental work of dream formation into two achievements: «die Herstellung der Traumgedanken und die Umwandlung derselben zum Trauminhalt» [GW II/III:510]. It calls the second achievement the «eigentliche Traumarbeit» (dream-work proper) [GW II/III:511].

In 1901 the term is first formally defined in the passages read [GW II/III:654]. That text describes the third operation in several ways: - the need for visual preparation: «Der Trauminhalt besteht zumeist aus anschaulichen Situationen; die Traumgedanken müssen also vorerst eine Zurichtung erfahren, welche sie für diese Darstellungsweise brauchbar macht» [GW II/III:672]; - «anschaulichen Zurichtung des psychischen Materials» [GW II/III:679]; - “Dramatisierung”, the «Verwandlung eines Gedankens in eine Situation» [GW II/III:666].

I read these as names for one operation, not as an additional operation [ii]. The 1901 text then closes the list: «Andere als die vier erwähnten Tätigkeiten sind bei der Traumarbeit nicht zu entdecken» [GW II/III:680].

The 1901 text also names a minimal transformation as dream-work. Of the first category of dreams, those «in denen manifester und latenter Inhalt zusammenfallen», it first says that the dream-work «also erspart scheint» [GW II/III:656]. Later the same text says: «Auch in dieser Gruppe wird also eine Art Umwandlung — die man als Traumarbeit bezeichnen darf — nicht völlig vermißt» [GW II/III:660].

That transformation is the replacement of a thought in the optative by a present-tense presentation. It recurs in 1905 («Zunächst macht sie den Schritt vom Optativ zum Präsens» [GW VI:185]) and in 1916 («nämlich die Umsetzung der Wunschform in die Realität» [GW XI:174]). It links aspect (a) to the wish-fulfilment of aspect (c) [ii].

On my reading [ii], page 660 corrects the hedged first claim of 656 («scheint») rather than contradicting it. Read strictly, however, the two pages differ on whether the dream-work is present in this group at all. That difference concerns the scope of the term, not the ranking or count of operations, so it is held as its own item under “Tensions held”.

In 1905 there are three “great achievements” and a fourth that is left unnamed [GW VI:187]. In 1913 only condensation and displacement are named, and only as examples [GW VIII:397]. In 1916 there are four operations again [GW XI:185].

In 1923 secondary revision is excluded: «Dieser letzte Vorgang gehört eigentlich nicht mehr der Traumarbeit an» [GW XIII:217].

In 1933 the core is declared unchanged: «Das alles hätte ich Ihnen schon vor fünfzehn Jahren sagen können» [GW XV:22]. The operations are restated: - images by regression: «Vorstellungen in visuelle Bilder umgesetzt» [GW XV:19]; - condensation: «zu neuen Einheiten verdichtet» [GW XV:20]; - displacement: «Verschiebung oder Akzentübertragung» [GW XV:21]; - secondary revision: «die sogenannte sekundäre Bearbeitung» [GW XV:21].

What 1933 calls an inexhaustible subject is the dream-work’s modes of representation, not the list of operations: «Die Darstellungsweisen der Traumarbeit, ein reizvoller und kaum zu erschöpfender Stoff» [GW XV:26].

In 1940 the definition is restated [GW XVII:88], and representability is no longer attested.

Across these texts two things stay constant: the direction (latent to manifest) and the claim that condensation and displacement belong to the dream-work. What changes is the list of operations and its boundaries.

Freud’s own or taken over. The passages read present the term as Freud’s own naming: - «Alles, was wir die „Traumarbeit“ geheißen haben» [GW II/III:597]; - «werde ich die Traumarbeit nennen» [GW II/III:654]; - «Halten wir an der Begriffsbestimmung fest, daß „Traumarbeit“ die Überführung der Traumgedanken in den Trauminhalt bezeichnet» [GW II/III:680].

He also presents the task as new: «An uns tritt darum auch als neu eine Aufgabe heran, die es vordem nicht gegeben hat» [GW II/III:283].

The sentence on Scherner, «fast alles, was er der Traumarbeit zuschreibt, ist der Tätigkeit des bei Tag regsamen Unbewußten zuzurechnen» [GW II/III:597], applies Freud’s term to what Scherner attributed to the dream and reassigns it. It does not show Scherner using the word.

Of secondary revision, Freud says it is the moment of the dream-work «welches von den meisten Autoren bemerkt und in seiner Bedeutung gewürdigt worden ist» [GW II/III:505]. He adds that for those authors it was «dieses einzig erkannte Moment der Traumbildung» [GW II/III:506].

Abandoned or kept. Kept in every text read, 1900–1940. In 1933 it is explicitly said to be unchanged [GW XV:22], and it is restated in 1940 [GW XVII:88].

Not to be confused with. - The dream thoughts. «Die latenten Traumgedanken sind der Stoff, den die Traumarbeit zum manifesten Traum umbildet» [GW XI:229]. - The work of interpretation. In 1901 it is called «Analysenarbeit» [GW II/III:654]; in 1916 «Deutungsarbeit» [GW XI:174]. - Distortion. Distortion is the dream-work’s result: «Traumentstellung, welche das Werk der Traumarbeit ist» [GW II/III:685]. - Symbolism. Symbolism is not produced by the dream-work, but the dream-work uses it. - In 1900 the dream-work «mit dieser Art von Ersetzung überhaupt nichts Originelles leistet» [GW II/III:351], and «daß man keine besondere symbolisierende Tätigkeit der Seele bei der Traumarbeit anzunehmen braucht» [GW II/III:354]. - In 1901 symbolism is something «daß sie nicht von der Traumarbeit hergestellt wird». It belongs, probably, to unconscious thinking, «welches der Traumarbeit das Material zur Verdichtung, Verschiebung und Dramatisierung liefert» [GW II/III:699]. - The later texts place symbols differently. In 1923 symbols were found «Beim Studium der durch die Traumarbeit geschaffenen Ausdrucksweise» [GW XIII:218]. In 1933 the use of symbols is listed among the changes the dream thoughts undergo: «wenn die Darstellung von gewissen Objekten und Vorgängen durch Symbole, die dem bewußten Denken fremd geworden sind, in reichem Ausmaß verwendet wird» [GW XV:20]. These later pages do not say whether the dream-work produces the symbols.

HOLD TENSION.

Sense 1, aspect (b): The dream-work as a case of unconscious, primary-process working (1900 to 1940)

In Freud’s words. - «daß Gedanken, die sich als Ergebnisse der sekundären Denkarbeit darstellen, dem primären psychischen Vorgang verfallen, mit welcher Formel wir jetzt die zum Traume und zu den hysterischen Symptomen führende Arbeit beschreiben können.» [GW II/III:608] (that thoughts which present themselves as results of secondary thought-work fall under the primary psychical process, the formula by which we can now describe the work leading to the dream and to hysterical symptoms.) - «Die Traumarbeit ist also im wesentlichen ein Fall von unbewusster Bearbeitung vorbewusster Gedankenvorgänge.» [GW XVII:90] (The dream-work is thus essentially a case of unconscious working-over of preconscious thought processes.)

What it means. The operations of aspect (a) are re-placed in a theory of the psychical apparatus. Thoughts that preconscious cathexis has abandoned are filled by unconscious wishful energy and «unterliegen ... dem primären psychischen Vorgang» [GW II/III:610]. The operations are then not errors but «die von einer Hemmung befreiten Arbeitsweisen des psychischen Apparats» [GW II/III:611].

The dream-work thereby becomes the source of knowledge about unconscious process: - 1905: «Wir kennen die Charaktere des unbewußten Denkens ... bisher nur aus den Ergebnissen eben der „Traumarbeit“» [GW VI:184]; - 1913: «Die Traumarbeit zwingt uns, eine unbewußte psychische Tätigkeit anzunehmen» [GW VIII:397].

On my reading, “unbewußt” is used in the 1905, 1913 and 1940 passages cited here in the systematic sense [ii]. The wording «System der unbewußten Seelentätigkeit» [GW VIII:397] and the Es/Ich context at [GW XVII:89–90] license this reading.

When and where. Chapter VI of the 1900 book already speaks of dream formation in terms of unconscious thinking: «daß es sich um unbewußtes Denken handelt» [GW II/III:287]. On the same page this is set against «absichtlichen, von Bewußtsein begleiteten Nachdenken». On that wording the sense is descriptive: not accompanied by consciousness.

The same 1900 text uses the descriptive wording for the correct dream thoughts themselves: «sie gehören unserem nicht bewußt gewordenen Denken an» [GW II/III:510]. It also sets those thoughts apart from the dream-work proper [GW II/III:511]. In these chapter VI passages, then, the word also covers the correct dream thoughts, and it is not shown to carry the systematic, primary-process sense of this aspect.

The same applies to the phrase that the dream-work follows paths «die sie im unbewußten Denken bereits gebahnt vorfindet» [GW II/III:351]; the pages do not fix its sense. These passages are therefore at most a precursor of aspect (b) [ii]. The chapter VI pages read do not use the primary-process framing.

That framing appears in the seventh chapter. There, «daß an der Traumbildung zweierlei wesensverschiedene psychische Vorgänge beteiligt sind» [GW II/III:602], and the second is the «eigentliche Traumarbeit» [GW II/III:603].

Later texts continue the framing: - 1905: the dream-work is located «in die Region des Unbewußten», with the cautious wording «möchte ich mich etwa der Aufstellung getrauen» [GW VI:188]; - 1917: the agent of condensation and displacement is named as «der psychische Primärvorgang» [GW X:418]; - 1933: the dream-work gave «den ersten Einblick in die Vorgänge ... im unbewußten System» [GW XV:17], and its operations are called «Mechanismen — wir getrauen uns nicht zu sagen: Denkvorgänge» [GW XV:18].

In 1940 this description is given as what the dream-work “im wesentlichen” is [GW XVII:90], two pages after the process definition [GW XVII:88]. Reading this as the 1940 definition is my interpretation [ii]. The theory then uses condensation and displacement «zur Charakteristik des dem Es zugeschriebenen Primärvorgangs» [GW XVII:91].

Freud’s own or taken over. In the pages read this is Freud’s own sense. Earlier formulas, the «Rückkehr auf den embryonalen Standpunkt» and Havelock Ellis’s phrase, are treated as «glückliche Vorwegnahmen» (happy anticipations) [GW II/III:596]. The concept of the unconscious on which the sense rests is presented in 1905 as shared with Lipps [GW VI:184–185]. Its gloss there is «daß das Unbewußte etwas ist, was man wirklich nicht weiß» [GW VI:185].

Abandoned or kept. Kept. In 1940 it is given as an “im wesentlichen” characterisation of the dream-work [ii].

Not to be confused with. - Secondary revision. Sekundäre Bearbeitung (secondary revision) is not Sekundärvorgang (secondary process) [ii]. “Sekundärvorgang” [GW II/III:607] and «sekundären Denkarbeit» [GW II/III:608] name the inhibited process that produces the correct thoughts. Neither names an operation of the dream-work.

The terms are not wholly unrelated, however [ii]. In 1900 the function behind secondary revision is identified, with a hedge, as normal thinking: «Es ist also wohl keine andere psychische Instanz als unser normales Denken» [GW II/III:504]. The same text also speaks of «daß die sekundäre Traumarbeit auch für einen Beitrag zur plastischen Intensität der einzelnen Traumgebilde verantwortlich zu machen ist» [GW II/III:505]. Normal thinking is the thinking whose mode 607 calls the secondary process, so in 1900 secondary revision is carried out by such thinking. - The phantasy activity that forms the dream thoughts. Aspect (b) is not the same as the unconscious phantasy activity that forms the dream thoughts: «Die Traumarbeit mußten wir von dieser Tätigkeit als etwas gänzlich Verschiedenes und weit mehr Gebundenes absondern» [GW II/III:597].

Sense 1, aspect (c): The dream-work as wish-fulfilling, sleep-preserving performance (1900 to 1940)

In Freud’s words. - «Die Funktion der Traumarbeit ist immer nur die, den Schlaf zu erhalten.» [GW VIII:398] (The function of the dream-work is always only to preserve sleep.) - «Sondern sie nimmt regelmäßig etwas hinzu, was nicht zu den latenten Gedanken des Tages gehört, was aber der eigentliche Motor der Traumbildung ist.» [GW XI:230] (Rather it regularly adds something that does not belong to the latent thoughts of the day, but which is the real motor of dream formation.) - «Diese Ersetzung des Anspruches durch Wunscherfüllung bleibt die wesentliche Leistung der Traumarbeit.» [GW XVII:92] (This replacement of the demand by wish-fulfilment remains the essential achievement of the dream-work.)

What it means. Here the same process is characterised by its aim. In 1913 it presents a wish as fulfilled by hallucinatory means [GW VIII:398].

In 1916 the aim is stated as a finding from children’s dreams: «Aus den Kinderträumen haben wir erfahren, die Traumarbeit beabsichtige die Beseitigung eines den Schlaf störenden seelischen Reizes durch eine Wunscherfüllung» [GW XI:218]. The lecture still has to show that the same holds for distorted dreams, so this sentence does not by itself state the aim for all dreams.

When and where. - 1900. The dream-work already effects wish-fulfilment. «in der Darstellung dieses unerwünschtesten aller Gedanken durch eine Wunscherfüllung hat die Traumarbeit ihr Meisterstück geleistet» [GW II/III:457]. «Die Traumarbeit verwendet das Träumen selbst als eine Form der Ablehnung und bezeugt damit die Einsicht, daß der Traum eine Wunscherfüllung ist» [GW II/III:344]. With an incoming stimulus: «benützt die Traumarbeit den anlangenden Reiz rasch zur Herstellung einer Wunscherfüllung, als ob sie denken würde (dies ist durchaus figürlich zu nehmen)» [GW II/III:500]. - 1901. Part of the contrast between manifest and latent content is traced to wish-fulfilment, and the next sentence speaks of «eine andere Leistung der Traumarbeit» [GW II/III:661]. This implies that the wish-fulfilling reversal also counts as a performance of the dream-work [ii]. The dream-work is also said to spare anxiety in the second class of dreams: «sie ist nur in den Träumen der zweiten Klasse durch die Traumarbeit erspart worden» [GW II/III:688]. - 1905. «für den Schlaf unschädlich gemacht» [GW VI:183]. - 1913. What is new is the claim that preserving sleep is the dream-work’s “only” function [GW VIII:398]. - 1916. The dream-work “adds” the unconscious wish [GW XI:230]. - 1917. Wish-phantasy formation and regression to hallucination are «die wesentlichsten Stücke der Traumarbeit» [GW X:420]. - 1940. The function is a task that «das schlafende Ich ... auf sich nimmt» [GW XVII:92], and the replacement of the demand by wish-fulfilment is the “essential achievement” [GW XVII:92].

The 1900 text already records failures of the dream-work, but most of them concern other operations, not the wish-fulfilling function: - the dream-work failed to produce a single wording: «Es ist der Traumarbeit nicht gelungen, einen einheitlichen, aber dann zweideutigen Wortlaut für die Traumgedanken herzustellen» [GW II/III:323]; - confused dream parts are those «wo die Kraft dieser Leistung versagt hat», said of secondary revision [GW II/III:505].

In the 1900 pages read, two passages bear on the wish-fulfilling conversion itself, and both concern affect. The dream «kann ... nur die den Affekt begleitende Vorstellung in ihr Gegenteil verkehren, nicht immer auch den Affekt selbst» [GW II/III:457]. A footnote on GW II/III:463, whose date the pages read do not fix, reports a case in which the conversion succeeded while the affect persisted: «daß es der Traumarbeit gelungen ist, ihren Stoff in eine Wunscherfüllung zu verwandeln, während der dazugehörige Affekt sich auch im Schlafzustand unverändert durchsetzt» [GW II/III:463].

Failure of the wish-fulfilling aim is stated directly in the following passages: - In 1916 the dream-work «hat ihren Zweck nicht erreicht» [GW XI:220]. - In 1933, in traumatic neurosis, «versagt die Leistung seiner Traumarbeit» [GW XV:31]. - Also in 1933 the formula is reworded: «der Traum ist der Versuch einer Wunscherfüllung» [GW XV:30]. Its subject is the dream, not the dream-work. - The 1940 text repeats “Versuch”: «der Traum sei jedes Mal ein Versuch, die Schlafstörung durch Wunscherfüllung zu beseitigen» [GW XVII:93].

This list is not exhaustive and does not establish a first date. Page 694 of the 1901 text lies within the coverage read and bears on the failure of the dream’s sleep-preserving function, in a sentence whose subject is the dream, not the dream-work. It is not quoted here.

Freud’s own or taken over. Freud’s own in the pages read. The 1913 dictum «„Der Traum ist der Hüter des Schlafes."» is printed in quotation marks without attribution, and its subject is the dream, not the dream-work [GW VIII:398]. No claim is made here that 1913 is the image’s earliest occurrence.

The same image appears in Freud’s own voice in, among others, these passages read: - 1916: «Wir haben den Traum mit dem Nachtwächter oder Schlafwächter verglichen» [GW XI:223]; - 1940: «er sei also der Hüter des Schlafes» [GW XVII:93].

This list is not complete. Page 691 of the 1901 text lies within the coverage read and bears on this image, again with the dream as subject. It is not quoted here.

Abandoned or kept. Kept. The 1933 rewording (“Versuch”) has the dream as its subject and is repeated in 1940 [GW XV:30], [GW XVII:93]. This entry makes no claim about how Freud himself weighs the rewording.

Not to be confused with. The wish itself. - In 1933 the unconscious impulse is «der eigentliche Schöpfer des Traums» [GW XV:18]. That sentence does not mention the dream-work. Reading it as saying that the energy comes from the impulse and not from the dream-work is my interpretation [ii]. - In 1916 the dream-work is said to add the wish: «Diese unentbehrliche Zutat ist der gleichfalls unbewußte Wunsch» [GW XI:230]. The wish, for its part, supplies the energy: «Die Rolle des Kapitalisten spielt für die Traumbildung immer nur der unbewußte Wunsch; er gibt die psychische Energie für die Traumbildung» [GW XI:232]. The texts do not state how “adding” the wish relates to the wish supplying the energy. - In the 1916 example the substitution is «das Werk der Wunscherfüllung» [GW XI:226], and the sentence does not mention the dream-work.

Sense 2: The whole of the night's dreaming (1900 only)

In Freud’s words. «wäre dann bloß ein Rest der gesamten Traumarbeit, welche wohl den Traumgedanken an Umfang gleichkäme, wenn wir sie eben vollständig erinnern könnten» [GW II/III:285] (would then be merely a remnant of the entire dream-work, which might equal the dream thoughts in extent if only we could remember it completely.)

What it means. On my reading, “Traumarbeit” here names a quantity of dreamed material that could in principle be remembered, rather than a transforming process [ii]. The single sentence also allows another reading: it may refer to the process taken in its full extent. The words «vollständig erinnern» favour the first reading, but they do not exclude the second.

When and where. It occurs once, on the same page where the process sense also appears: «Die Annahme einer Verdichtung in der Traumarbeit» [GW II/III:285].

Freud’s own or taken over. It occurs inside an objection. Freud grants part of the objection before denying that it touches condensation [GW II/III:285]: «Die Annahme einer Verdichtung in der Traumarbeit wird überdies von der Möglichkeit des Traumvergessens nicht berührt» [GW II/III:285]. The pages do not settle whose voice the usage represents.

Abandoned or kept. It is not found again in the passages read [ii].

Not to be confused with. Sense 1 on the same page [GW II/III:285].

How the senses relate

Aspect (b) grows out of aspect (a) within one book. The operations listed in chapter VI are recast in chapter VII as primary-process modes of working, and the text refers back to the earlier chapter: «Den letzteren haben wir schon im Abschnitt VI als die eigentliche Traumarbeit abgesondert» [GW II/III:603]. Aspect (b) does not replace aspect (a). The two coexist: in 1940 the process definition [GW XVII:88] stands two pages from the metapsychological characterisation [GW XVII:90].

Aspect (c) coexists with both and gains weight over time [ii]: - in 1900–1901 the dream-work already performs wish-fulfilment [GW II/III:344], [GW II/III:457], [GW II/III:661]; - in 1913 preserving sleep becomes its “only” function [GW VIII:398]; - in 1940 the replacement of the demand by wish-fulfilment becomes its “essential achievement” [GW XVII:92].

The texts link aspect (a) to symptom formation from 1900 onwards [GW II/III:603], [GW II/III:684], [GW XVII:94].

Sense 2 is not found again after its single occurrence [ii].

The texts never state how the three aspects of sense 1 fit together as one process seen as operation, law and function. Any such model remains [iii].

Tensions held

  • What the dream-work essentially is or does. At different dates the texts mark different aspects of sense 1 as essential:
  • aspect (a): «die Traumarbeit bestehe wesentlich in der Umsetzung von Gedanken in ein halluzinatorisches Erleben» [GW XI:218];
  • aspect (b): «im wesentlichen ein Fall von unbewusster Bearbeitung vorbewusster Gedankenvorgänge» [GW XVII:90];
  • aspect (c): «bleibt die wesentliche Leistung der Traumarbeit» [GW XVII:92].

The two 1940 formulas stand two pages apart. Other “wesentlich” formulas do not choose among the aspects: - «geradezu das wesentliche Stück der Traumarbeit» [GW II/III:313] ranks one operation. - «die wesentlichsten Stücke der Traumarbeit» [GW X:420] names wish-phantasy formation and regression to hallucination, which straddle aspects (a) and (c). - Three formulas rank the dream-work above the dream thoughts. - The first is «sie allein ist das Wesentliche am Traum» [GW II/III:511], the continuation of a footnote that begins on 510. The footnote marks itself as later than the original text: «Ich fand es früher einmal so außerordentlich schwierig, die Leser an die Unterscheidung von manifestem Trauminhalt und latenten Traumgedanken zu gewöhnen» [GW II/III:510]. Its exact date is not fixed by the pages read. - The others are «Das einzig Wesentliche am Traum ist die Traumarbeit» [GW XI:229] and «daß das Wesentliche am Traum der Prozeß der Traumarbeit ist» [GW XV:7].

The 1900 formula «sonst nichts leistet als eine Übersetzung» [GW II/III:447] limits what the dream-work does. It is not a claim about its essence. HOLD TENSION.

  • Translation only, or something added. In 1900: «sonst nichts leistet als eine Übersetzung der Traumgedanken» [GW II/III:447]. In 1901 the dream-work is «nicht schöpferisch» [GW II/III:680], and in 1923 it is denied a «„schöpferischen" Charakter» [GW XIII:217]. In 1916, against these: «daß die Traumarbeit sich nie darauf beschränkt, diese Gedanken in die Ihnen bekannte archaische oder regressive Ausdrucksweise zu übersetzen» [GW XI:229].

The 1916 position is not one-sided. The same lecture series also limits the dream-work: «Mit den aufgezählten Leistungen ist ihre Tätigkeit erschöpft; mehr als verdichten, verschieben, plastisch darstellen und das Ganze dann einer sekundären Bearbeitung unterziehen, kann sie nicht» [GW XI:185]. The limiting statement (Lecture XI) and the “adds” statement (Lecture XIV) stand within one series, so the tension also runs inside the 1916 text.

Within 1900 itself: - the dream-work creates contacts «wo sie nicht vorhanden sind» [GW II/III:345]; - the statement that it «nichts Originelles leistet» is restricted to symbolic substitution: «mit dieser Art von Ersetzung» [GW II/III:351]; - of secondary revision, 1900 says: «ich möchte diesem vierten Momente also die Fähigkeit, schöpferisch neue Beiträge zum Traume zu liefern, nicht peremptorisch absprechen» [GW II/III:495].

HOLD TENSION.

  • Scope of the term in 1901. Of dreams «in denen manifester und latenter Inhalt zusammenfallen», the text first says that the dream-work «also erspart scheint» [GW II/III:656]. It later says: «Auch in dieser Gruppe wird also eine Art Umwandlung — die man als Traumarbeit bezeichnen darf — nicht völlig vermißt» [GW II/III:660].

Reading 660 as correcting the hedged first claim is my interpretation [ii]. Taken strictly, the two pages differ on whether the term applies to this group. HOLD TENSION.

  • Is secondary revision part of the dream-work? It is included in 1900 [GW II/III:505] and 1916 [GW XI:185]. The other texts pull in different directions:
  • 1900 also reserves an «eigentliche Traumarbeit» that differs from waking thought [GW II/III:511]. It compares the function behind secondary revision with waking thought: «Unser waches (vorbewußtes) Denken benimmt sich gegen ein beliebiges Wahrnehmungsmaterial ganz ebenso wie die in Frage stehende Funktion gegen den Trauminhalt» [GW II/III:503]. It then concludes, with a hedge: «Es ist also wohl keine andere psychische Instanz als unser normales Denken» [GW II/III:504].
  • In 1917 secondary revision is what «jeder Wahrnehmungsinhalt» undergoes [GW X:420].
  • In 1923 it is excluded [GW XIII:217].
  • In 1933 it comes after the dream is «fast fertiggemacht» [GW XV:21]. A few sentences later the text continues «auch die Traumarbeit nicht immer gleich energisch verfährt» [GW XV:22]. This can be read as placing secondary revision either inside or outside the dream-work; on my reading the question is left open in 1933 [ii].
  • In 1940 the text says that «das Ergebnis der Traumarbeit ein Kompromiss ist», and that in distortion and in «(sekundäre Bearbeitung) ist der Einfluss der noch nicht gelähmten Ichorganisation zu erkennen» [GW XVII:90]. Reading this as placing secondary revision within the compromise that the dream-work produces is my interpretation [ii].

HOLD TENSION.

  • Who performs the work. In 1940 the unconscious works over preconscious material [GW XVII:90], while the sleeping ego «die Aufgabe der Traumarbeit auf sich nimmt» [GW XVII:92]. In 1901 distortion is the dream-work’s product, «welche das Werk der Traumarbeit ist» [GW II/III:685]. In the same text the censorship is named as its main motive: «Wer an dem Gesichtspunkte der Zensur als dem Hauptmotiv der Traumentstellung festhält» [GW II/III:695]. HOLD TENSION.

  • Regression inside or after the dream-work. In 1905 the text speaks of «was ich als die „Regression“ der Traumarbeit bezeichnet habe» [GW VI:185], but it also names «sodann die eigentliche Traumarbeit im Unbewußten» as a stage before regression [GW VI:188]. HOLD TENSION.

  • Mechanism versus intention. The dream-work «denkt, rechnet, urteilt überhaupt nicht» [GW II/III:511], yet «hat die Traumarbeit ihr Meisterstück geleistet» [GW II/III:457]. In 1933 it consists of “Mechanismen” [GW XV:18], and it also shows a «Bemühung ... die ... Enttäuschung in Gewährung verwandeln will» [GW XV:30]. HOLD TENSION.

  • Identity with symptom formation. The 1900 text speaks first of «vollen Identität» [GW II/III:603] and then of «die größte Analogie im wesentlichen» [GW II/III:612]. In 1901 the shared part is limited: «Verdichtung und vor allem Verschiebung sind niemals fehlende Charaktere auch dieser anderen Prozesse. Die Umarbeitung aufs Anschauliche bleibt hingegen der Traumarbeit eigentümlich» [GW II/III:684]. HOLD TENSION.

  • Ranking and count of operations.

  • Ranking. In 1900 displacement is ranked first in a sentence framed as a supposition: «Der Vorgang, den wir so supponieren, ist geradezu das wesentliche Stück der Traumarbeit» [GW II/III:313]. Within the single 1901 text, dramatisation and condensation are «der wichtigste und eigentümlichste Charakter» [GW II/III:666], while displacement is «der weitaus auffälligsten unter den Einzelleistungen der Traumarbeit» [GW II/III:684].
  • Count. The 1901 list is closed at four [GW II/III:680]. In 1905 there are three great achievements and an unnamed fourth [GW VI:187]. In 1933 the operations are restated [GW XV:19–21]; what is called inexhaustible there is the study of the modes of representation [GW XV:26], not the list.
  • The optative-to-present transformation. The texts place it differently at different dates:
    • 1901: the dream of the infantile type «stellt diese Erfüllung als real und gegenwärtig dar», in material that consists mainly «aus Situationen und meist visuellen Sinnesbildern» [GW II/III:660]. The text then asks of confused dreams whether the conversion there too concerned an optative, «wiewohl wir nicht wissen können, ob sie auch hier einen Optativ betraf» [GW II/III:661]. It names the «Verwandlung eines Gedankens in eine Situation» “Dramatisierung” [GW II/III:666]. Reading the optative step as falling under dramatisation is licensed by these passages [ii].
    • 1905: the «Es ist» is tied to regression: «Dies „Es ist“ ist zur halluzinatorischen Darstellung bestimmt, was ich als die „Regression“ der Traumarbeit bezeichnet habe» [GW VI:185].
    • 1916: it is listed beside, and apart from, conversion into visual images: «nämlich die Umsetzung der Wunschform in die Realität und zumeist auch die der Gedanken in visuelle Bilder» [GW XI:174].

HOLD TENSION.

  • Condensation and displacement as stable members. These two are named in every text read except 1916 Lecture XIV, which does not cover the operations. CONFIRMED.

What this entry does not claim

  • It does not claim that "Rücksicht auf Darstellbarkeit" was abandoned. The phrase is absent from the 1913, 1916, 1923, 1933 and 1940 pages read, and that absence is all that is stated.
  • It does not identify the unnamed 1905 fourth achievement [GW VI:187] with secondary revision [iii].
  • It does not date individual sentences of the printed Traumdeutung. The printed text cites works of 1905, 1909, 1910 and 1914 (for example [GW II/III:309], [GW II/III:316], [GW II/III:349]). The footnote «Die Traumarbeit ist es, die diese Form herstellt» [GW II/III:511] marks itself as a later addition to the 1900 text by looking back («früher einmal» [GW II/III:510]), but its exact date is not fixed by the pages read.
  • It does not say what the dream-work does to affects beyond the 1900 and 1933 passages ([GW II/III:470], [GW XV:21]).
  • It draws on no Standard Edition terms, other works, or secondary literature.
